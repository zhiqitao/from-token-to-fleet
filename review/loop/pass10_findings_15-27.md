## FINDINGS

**Severity: MAJOR | Location: Preface (render/latex/preface.tex lines ~112–125) vs Back matter "Sources, Method, and Reproducibility" (sources.tex lines 10–29) | Problem:** The book publishes two *different* definitions of its own two-axis evidence taxonomy. The Preface puts **ILLUSTRATIVE on the claim-type (epistemic-status) axis** and describes the source axis as only `[1P]` / `[2°]` / "not yet anchored", with status categories FACT / DERIVED / HYPOTHESIS / **ILLUSTRATIVE**, and no `[ASSUMPTION]`, `[UNRESOLVED]`, `[LAB]`, or `[canonical scenario]`. The back-matter Sources chapter instead puts **ILLUSTRATIVE on the provenance (Axis 1)** and adds `[ASSUMPTION]` (which "pairs with a provenance of [ILLUSTRATIVE]"), `[UNRESOLVED]`, `[LAB]`, and `[canonical scenario]` to the axes; its status axis is FACT / DERIVED / ASSUMPTION / HYPOTHESIS / UNRESOLVED. (A third variant exists in design/book-architecture.md §5/§14 — `[VERIFY]`, MEASURED, EXPERIMENT — which matches neither published one.) | **Why it matters:** This is the book's core methodological selling point, and it is stated two different ways in the two places a reader meets it (Preface first, then the Sources chapter). A reader who reads the Preface will conclude `[ILLUSTRATIVE][DERIVED]` is a double-status label (ILLUSTRATIVE + DERIVED both "claim-type"), whereas the Sources chapter and the body use `[ILLUSTRATIVE]` as *provenance* and `[DERIVED]` as *status* — exactly the axis-conflation the book repeatedly warns against. Because `[ILLUSTRATIVE]`, `[ASSUMPTION]`, and `[canonical scenario]` are all present in the body but placed on different axes in the two authoritative statements, the labeling system is not self-consistent. | **Recommended change:** Harmonize on one taxonomy (the back-matter Sources chapter, since it is the published statement that the body actually follows) and rewrite the Preface's "Architect's Evidence Discipline" paragraph to match it exactly — or state explicitly that the Preface is a simplified preview and defer the full five-part provenance + five-part status axes to the Sources chapter.

**Severity: MAJOR | Location: Ch16 §6 "Architecture Consequence" (chapter-16.md line 117) vs §3 "Reading the result" (line 83) and Table 16-1 (lines 87–91) | Problem:** §6 asserts the 70B 8×H100 self-host option "wins on TCO at canonical volume", but the chapter's own worked arithmetic concludes the opposite. §3 states self-hosted (~$5.7/1K) is "roughly twice the cloud-scaled rate ($2.8/1K)" and "**loses to scale-to-load cloud** … Cloud-scaled (~$2.8/1K) is the cheapest at this volume"; Table 16-1 likewise gives cloud-on-demand $2.8/1K vs self-host $5.7/1K. | **Why it matters:** The chapter's headline TCO lesson (the whole reason it exists) is "self-host loses to cloud-scaled here, and only *beats the managed API*," yet the Architecture-Consequence section (the takeaway the reader carries) declares self-host the TCO winner. This is a same-chapter claim-vs-derivation contradiction on the single most consequential conclusion in the TCO chapter. | **Recommended change:** Rephrase §6 to match §3/16-1 — e.g. "wins on TCO versus the managed API at canonical volume, but loses to scale-to-load cloud; the surviving recommendation depends on the staff/ops term and the elasticity assumption" — and state that the capability/SLO screen is passed while the TCO gate is a *conditional* win.

**Severity: MODERATE | Location: Ch22 §3 Step 1 and Table 22-1 (chapter-22.md line 25 "These numbers are not assumed; they are measured [1P]"; line 58 "9,200 ÷ 300 [1P][DERIVED]") | Problem:** The chapter labels the canonical workload's token counts as **"measured [1P]"** and tags the ~30× ratio `[1P][DERIVED]`. Per the book's own taxonomy (sources.tex), `[1P]` = vendor first-party (model card/spec); the canonical 9.2K-in / 300-out counts are the book's author-defined `[canonical scenario]` / `[ILLUSTRATIVE]` inputs (sources.tex line 14: "author-defined, *not* external first-party evidence"). Indeed the same Table 22-1 labels the identical canonical inputs in adjacent rows `[ILLUSTRATIVE][DERIVED]` (lines 56–57, 59–62), so one table carries two provenances for the same scenario and one of them (`[1P]`) is wrong by the book's own definition. | **Why it matters:** This is precisely the provenance-axon error the book's discipline is designed to prevent, and it appears in the "flagship" chapter (Ch22, the canonical loop that the Preface calls the spine). It will mislead a reader into thinking the 9.2K/300 counts are vendor-measured facts rather than the book's illustrative scenario inputs. | **Recommended change:** Re-label Step 1 and the `[1P]` rows in Table 22-1 to `[canonical scenario]` (or `[ILLUSTRATIVE][ASSUMPTION]`), consistent with sources.tex and with the chapter's own adjacent `[ILLUSTRATIVE][DERIVED]` rows.

**Severity: MODERATE | Location: Ch22 §2 Mental Model (chapter-22.md lines 15–18) vs Ch2 §2 (chapter-02.md lines 60–64) | Problem:** Ch22 restates the identical water-metaphor explanation of prefill/decode that Ch2 already gave: Ch2's "Prefill is compute-bound. It is like turning on a massive industrial fire hose … limited entirely by the raw horsepower of the pump (compute/FLOPs) … the pipe is wide open" and "Decode … emptying a … pool through a … garden hose … the diameter of the hose (HBM bandwidth)" become Ch22's "prefill is like filling a bucket from a fire hose … limited by how hard the pump pushes water … the pipe is more than wide enough" and "Decode is like drinking from that bucket one sip at a time … limited by how fast water can be drawn through the straw (the memory-bandwidth feed rate)." Ch22 even opens its §1 with "We do not restate that here; this chapter builds on it" (line 9) — and then §2 restates exactly that in new wording. | **Why it matters:** This is a paragraph-level duplication of the book's own prefill/decode elucidation across chapters, and it is redundant precisely because Ch2 declared the split the "figure the rest of the book builds on" (Ch2 line 19). It is also the "X is like Y" tutorial-analogy device the book's voice convention explicitly forbids; here it crops up again in Ch22 in both the bucket/hose form (duplicated) and a new "two gears turning" simile. | **Recommended change:** In Ch22 §2, replace the water/gear analogies with a single one-line reminder that points to Ch2 §2 ("prefill is compute-bound, decode is bandwidth-bound — see Ch2"), keeping the chapter's actual addition (the *reasoning loop*), not a re-metaphorized version of an earlier chapter's imagery.

**Severity: MODERATE | Location: Ch22 §8 mini-case closing (chapter-22.md line ~126) | Problem:** The mini-case ends "Before passing this workload to **Chapter 23 (Memory)**". In the current book, Memory is **Chapter 7** and **Chapter 23 is "Working With Customers"**. The reference is both mis-numbered and mis-labeled (it appears to be a stale pre-renumber remnant from when memory sat later in the sequence). | **Why it matters:** A cross-chapter pointer that names the wrong chapter and the wrong topic sends the reader (and any downstream build tooling that treats the label as canonical) to the wrong chapter, and would confuse anyone following the Part VI-to-substrate handoff the sentence is trying to make. | **Recommended change:** Change to "**Chapter 7 (Memory)**" (or, if the intent is to hand the loop to the next discipline chapter, to "Chapter 23 (Working With Customers)" — but not "(Memory)").

**Severity: MODERATE | Location: Ch20 §1 Concept (chapter-20.md line 9) | Problem:** The chapter asserts "the **marginal cost per additional query declines** as the fleet grows, because fixed costs (model loading, infrastructure) are amortized across more concurrent requests." The chapter's own worked arithmetic models the opposite: each added host buys a constant ~2.1 req/s at a constant ~$20/hr, giving a *constant* marginal cost of ≈$9.5 per req/s per hour (§8, lines 111–113). Amortization lowers **average** cost, not **marginal** cost; the marginal cost is flat in the model the chapter uses, and Ch17 §7 frames the Nth host as *diminishing marginal benefit* (line 148). | **Why it matters:** This is a claim-strength / economics-terminology error in the chapter that exists to teach fleet economics: it states "marginal cost declines" as the central insight while its own calculus shows marginal cost is constant (and marginal *benefit* diminishes). The claim will read as authoritative and is not supported by the chapter's arithmetic. | **Recommended change:** Change to "the **average** cost per additional query declines as fixed costs amortize, while the **marginal** cost of each added host is roughly constant (~$9.5/req-s/hr) and the marginal *benefit* diminishes" — matching §8 and Ch17 §7.

**Severity: MODERATE | Location: Back matter taxonomy vs ch15–27 usage (sources.tex line 14 vs chapters 15–27) | Problem:** The Sources chapter defines `[canonical scenario]` as a distinct first-class **provenance** category, yet no chapter in 15–27 ever uses it as a label. The canonical workload's numbers are instead labeled `[ILLUSTRATIVE][DERIVED]` (ch15/17/20/24/25/26) or, in one case, `[1P][DERIVED]` (ch22). So the taxonomy advertises a provenance bucket that the body never invokes, and the bucket it actually uses (`[ILLUSTRATIVE]`) is the one that does the work of "this is our own teaching scenario." | **Why it matters:** It creates a terminological gap between the taxonomy the back matter describes and the one the body actually applies — a reader looking for the defined `[canonical scenario]` label never finds it, and the book's most important repeated quantity ("the canonical 70B RAG scenario") is tagged with a *different* provenance from the one the taxonomy reserves for it. | **Recommended change:** Either (a) use `[canonical scenario]` as the provenance on the canonical numbers (at least at first definition in ch15/ch17 and in ch25's ADR mini-cases), or (b) remove the `[canonical scenario]` bucket from the taxonomy and document that `[ILLUSTRATIVE]` covers the book's internal scenario. Pick one and apply it consistently.

**Severity: MINOR | Location: Ch21 §3 table note (chapter-21.md line 53) | Problem:** "The workload inputs are sourced from the canonical **canonical** scenario (Ch 4, Table 4-3) workload." — duplicated word "canonical canonical". | **Why it matters:** A typo in the book's flagship AI-Factory table note; reads as an editing artifact in a pass meant to converge. | **Recommended change:** "sourced from the canonical scenario (Ch 4, Table 4-3)…".

**Severity: MINOR | Location: Ch19 §4 scope note (chapter-19.md line ~120) restating Ch17 §8 (chapter-17.md line ~156) | Problem:** Ch19 describes Ch17's derivation as "1.08 s prefill of the 12,095-token context," but Ch17 holds the prefill at the **9.2K single-shot representative** (1.08 s) and explicitly notes a 12,095-token prefill "would scale to ~1.42 s" (giving W ≈ 14.2 s). Ch19's phrasing misstates Ch17's own premise, attributing the 1.08 s to a 12.1K-token context that Ch17 says would be ~1.42 s. | **Why it matters:** Ch19 is the in-chapter pointer to Ch17's full-service-time derivation; a reader cross-referencing the two will see Ch19 attribute 1.08 s to the grown context that Ch17 says scales to 1.42 s, seeding doubt about the W ≈ 13.8 s figure. | **Recommended change:** Change Ch19's parenthetical to "1.08 s prefill (held at the canonical 9.2K representative; the 12,095-token context would scale it to ~1.42 s)" to match Ch17 §8.

**Severity: MINOR | Location: Ch25 §4 "Measurement" (chapter-25.md line ~125) | Problem:** The ADR-derived "70B FP16 model footprint" uses "**~30 GB activations** … giving ≈170 GB total" while explicitly noting "it is below the ~64 GB runtime/workspace/NCCL reserve the body uses elsewhere — 140 GB weights + ~436 GB KV budget + ~64 GB reserve ≈ 640 GB." Two different activation/runtime figures (~30 GB vs ~64 GB) coexist for the same 8×H100 host, and Ch25 *adopts* the 30 GB figure in the ADR arithmetic while flagging the 64 GB one. | **Why it matters:** Terminology/service-time drift on the one quantity that reconciles to the 640 GB HBM envelope; a reader sees the ADR's 170 GB total but knows the body's reserve is 64 GB, so the two don't reconcile by ~34 GB. | **Recommended change:** Pick one activation/runtime figure and use it consistently (either use the 64 GB reserve here and reconcile, or say the 30 GB is a per-request working-set figure distinct from the 64 GB runtime/workspace reserve).

**Severity: MINOR | Location: Glossary completeness (render/latex/backmatter/glossary.tex) | Problem:** Several load-bearing terms/notation used in ch15–27 are not in the glossary: **RQO** (Reasoned Quality Optimization, used once in Ch25 §6, line ~156, and defined only by a bare parenthetical); **FSDP** (Ch26 §2.1 matrix "Training" row, line ~34); **CP** (context parallelism, named in the architecture's Ch10 scope but absent from the glossary's TP/PP/DP/EP entry, line ~38); and the arithmetic symbols **κ** (per-token KV footprint) and **ρ** (load-balancer throughput fraction) that ch17/ch20 use prominently. | **Why it matters:** The glossary is billed as "lets a reader gloss any term quickly"; a first-time reader hitting RQO or FSDP in Parts V–VI has no gloss, and the cryptic single-letter notation κ/ρ that carries the fleet arithmetic is never catalogued. | **Recommended change:** Add RQO, FSDP, CP to the acronym list and add κ (per-token KV footprint) and ρ (load-balancer efficiency fraction) to the "Notation used in the arithmetic" section.

## VERDICT
7 genuine new findings (2 MAJOR, 5 MODERATE, 5 MINOR), none duplicating the resolved numeric items of PASS-1..9. The two highest-value issues are whole-book/back-matter coherence rather than arithmetic: (1) the **two conflicting published definitions of the two-axis evidence taxonomy** (Preface puts ILLUSTRATIVE on the status axis and omits ASSUMPTION/UNRESOLVED/LAB/canonical-scenario; the Sources chapter puts ILLUSTRATIVE on the provenance axis and adds them) — a self-inconsistency in the book's central methodological claim; and (2) the **Ch16 §6 "wins on TCO" claim that contradicts Ch16 §3/Table 16-1's own finding that self-host loses to scale-to-load cloud**. The MODERATE items are: Ch22 mislabeling canonical inputs as "measured [1P]" (a provenance error by the book's own taxonomy), Ch22 §2 duplicating Ch2's fire-hose/bucket prefill-decode analogy (and re-using the forbidden "X is like Y" device), the Ch22 "Chapter 23 (Memory)" stale cross-reference (memory is Ch7), the Ch20 §1 "marginal cost declines" claim vs its own constant-marginal-cost arithmetic, and the unused `[canonical scenario]` provenance bucket. Minor items cover the ch21 duplicated word, ch19's misstatement of ch17's prefill scope, ch25's 30 GB vs 64 GB activation drift, and three glossary gaps (RQO, FSDP/CP, κ/ρ notation).
