## FINDINGS

**Severity: MEDIUM | Location: Ch15 §3 "Attention FLOP comparison" (chapter-15.md line ~27) vs Ch22 Fig 22.1 caption (chapter-22.md line ~70) | Problem:** The two chapters use different constants for the *same* per-layer attention FLOP cost. Ch15 computes it as `2 × L² × d_model` ≈ **1.39e12 FLOPs/layer** (per-head `2 × 9,200² × 128 ≈ 2.2e10`, ×64 heads), giving ≈ **1.1e14** across 80 layers. Ch22's Fig 22.1 caption states the "textbook" formula as `4·n_layers·L²·d` ≈ **2.22e14** (i.e. 2.77e12/layer). Standard attention (Q·Kᵀ then softmax·V) is `4·L²·d_model` per layer — **4**, not **2**. Ch15's per-head coefficient of 2 accounts for only one of the two matmuls and undercounts by exactly 2×; its total (1.1e14) is half of Ch22's (2.22e14). | **Why it matters:** A reader comparing the two chapters gets a 2× discrepancy for the identical quantity, and the chapter that the book elsewhere calls out as the "quadratic attention" discussion is the one that undercounts it. It undermines the cross-chapter consistency the convergence pass is meant to guarantee, even though the Ch15 figure is used qualitatively (to show attention grows dominant at long context) rather than in a sizing sum. | **Recommended change:** Harmonize on `4·L²·d_model` per layer: Ch15 per-layer ≈ 2.77e12, per-head ≈ 4.4e10 (×64), across 80 layers ≈ 2.22e14 — matching Ch22's textbook formula and Chapter 8. (Alternatively, state explicitly that Ch15 counts only the Q·Kᵀ matmul and note the ·V term is the same again.)

**Severity: MEDIUM | Location: Ch26 §3 capstone (chapter-26.md lines ~45, ~53) and §8 mini-case (lines ~91, ~95, ~97) | Problem:** The chapter's latency/QPS numbers are inconsistent with the book's own canonical service-time arithmetic. The capstone adopts a "p99 ≤ 800 ms" model-service SLO and the §8 mini-case reports the same 70B FP16 canonical host at **12 QPS with 450 ms p99** (then 210 ms, then 285 ms). But the book's canonical model (70B, 9.2K-in/300-out) has a ~**8.6 s** service time (1.08 s prefill + ~7.5 s decode of 300 tokens), which Ch20 §8 explicitly establishes makes sub-second end-to-end p99 **infeasible** (model execution alone exceeds any sub-100 ms budget by two orders of magnitude); and its per-host ceiling is C/W ≈ **2.1 req/s** (Ch17/20), so 12 QPS on one host is ~5.7× above that ceiling. Ch26's "p99 ≤ 800 ms" also cannot hold even as TTFT, since the canonical prefill alone is ~1.08 s > 800 ms. | **Why it matters:** This is the same class of "impossible SLO" that Ch20 spends its whole mini-case correcting. The chapter redeclares a sub-second p99 and reuses the canonical workload and host, directly contradicting Ch17/Ch20's discipline and the ~2.1 req/s + ~8.6 s service-time ceiling. A reader tracking the canonical numbers sees Ch26 break its own book's arithmetic. | **Recommended change:** Reconcile with Ch20: either (a) scope the sub-second figures strictly to time-to-first-token and check against the ~1.08 s prefill (which still exceeds 800 ms), (b) adopt the canonical p95-TTFT ≤ 2 s SLO (Ch4) and the ~2.1 req/s ceiling (Ch17/20) so 12 QPS/450 ms becomes ~2 QPS/~8.6 s, or (c) explicitly frame the capstone as a *different, small-output* workload (not the 9.2K-in/300-out canonical one) and state that divergence up front.

**Severity: MEDIUM | Location: Ch17 §4 "Cross-host interconnect budget" (chapter-17.md line ~103) vs §6 "Cross-host data infrastructure" (line ~134) | Problem:** The chapter computes two cross-host bandwidth quantities from **identical** inputs (φ=0.1, N=4, D=5 MB, λ_net=20 ms) that are physically incompatible. §4 gives per-request `B_per-req = D/(λ_net·φ·N)` = **625 MB/s (≈5 Gbps)** and calls it "per-request bandwidth drawn on the network," while §6 gives the aggregate minimum `B_min = φ·N·D/λ_net` = **100 MB/s (≈0.8 Gbps)**. A single cross-host request's required rate (≥250 MB/s at D/λ_net; the chapter's own 625 MB/s) exceeds the "minimum aggregate" (100 MB/s), which is impossible whenever more than one cross-host request is in flight. The two formulas are reciprocally tied by (φ·N)², so they cannot both be the right definition. | **Why it matters:** The section's purpose is to dimension the interconnect, but per-request bandwidth (5 Gbps) exceeds the derived fleet minimum (0.8 Gbps) for the same workload, so the "10 Gbps is ample" verdict and the §6 headroom conclusion rest on shared but mutually inconsistent formulas. | **Recommended change:** Unify on one clean definition and re-derive. E.g. per cross-host request = D/λ_net = 250 MB/s; aggregate = (number of concurrent cross-host requests) × D/λ_net, with concurrency from λ·W and φ. Then re-state the per-request figure (which should never exceed the aggregate) and the resulting 10 Gbps headroom consistently in both §4 and §6.

**Severity: LOW | Location: Ch27 Appendix A §5 (chapter-27.md line ~65) vs Ch15/17/19/25 vs Ch22 Table 22-1 line 66 | Problem:** The book carries two different "8-bit KV" constants, unlabeled as distinct. Ch15/17/19/25 use the vLLM-measured FP8 figure, 54% of BF16 = **~1.42 MB/token** (→ 13.4 GB at 9.5K). But Ch27 §5 says the KV constant is "~2.62 MB/token FP16, **~1.3 MB 8-bit**," and Ch22 Table 22-1's sensitivity note uses "**~12 GB under ideal byte-halving**" (9.2K × ~1.31 MB, i.e. 2.62/2). These are the *ideal byte-halving* constant (1.31 MB), not the measured FP8 54% constant (1.42 MB). | **Why it matters:** Two different "8-bit" values (1.31 vs 1.42 MB/token) float through the book without a label distinguishing measured-FP8 from ideal-byte-halving, so a reader can conflate the ~12 GB/13.4 GB and ~24.9/13.4 tonnages. This is exactly the KV constant the whole fleet-sizing chain (Ch17/19/25) depends on. | **Recommended change:** Add a one-line distinction where the 8-bit/FP8 constant first appears (and in Ch27 §5): measured FP8 KV ≈ 1.42 MB/token (54% of BF16, vLLM [S6]) vs nominal ideal byte-halving ≈ 1.31 MB/token, and state which is used where — or normalize both to 1.42.

**Severity: LOW | Location: Ch17 §8 mini-case (chapter-17.md line ~154) and Ch19 §4 scope note (chapter-19.md line ~120) — agentic full service time W | Problem:** The W ≈ 13.8 s that drives the headline fleet counts (552 in-flight at 40 rps → 141 hosts at 100 rps, etc.) is built from a **1.08 s** prefill, but for the T=3 scenario the grown 12,095-token context implies a prefill of **~1.42 s** (1.08 × 12,095/9,200). The chapter itself flags this ("a 12,095-token prefill would scale to ~1.42 s") yet still uses 1.08 s in the sum, so W and the in-flight/host counts it feeds are slightly understated (using 1.42 s would give W ≈ 14.2 s and ~145 hosts at 100 rps vs 141). The note acknowledges the choice, so it is documented, but the resulting numbers remain on the low side. | **Why it matters:** W is the single quantity that sizes the agentic fleet; using the canonical 9.2K prefill (1.08 s) rather than the T=3 scenario prefill (1.42 s) makes the fleet total a bit optimistic even though the divergence is annotated. | **Recommended change:** Either use the scaled prefill (~1.42 s) in W, giving W ≈ 14.2 s and updating the in-flight/host cells (552→568, 141→~145, 79→~81, etc.), or keep the canonical 1.08 s prefill but state explicitly that W is a lower bound on the agentic service time and that the resulting host counts are likewise lower bounds.

## VERDICT
5 new findings this pass (3 MEDIUM, 2 LOW), none duplicating the resolved PASS-1..8 items (whose residual edits are confirmed landed). The two most consequential are: (1) the Ch15-vs-Ch22 **2× attention-FLOP constant discrepancy** (2·L²·d vs the textbook 4·n_layers·L²·d) in the handbook's own attention-arithmetic discussion, and (2) the **Ch26 capstone/mini-case sub-second p99 and 12-QPS figures that contradict the book's canonical ~8.6 s service time and ~2.1 req/s per-host ceiling** — the very infeasibility Ch20 establishes. A third MEDIUM is the Ch17 §4-vs-§6 cross-host per-request-vs-aggregate bandwidth incompatibility (5 Gbps per request vs 0.8 Gbps aggregate for the same inputs). The two LOW items are the unlabeled 8-bit KV constant split (measured FP8 1.42 vs ideal byte-halving 1.31 MB/token) and the documented-but-lingering prefill-use in W (1.08 s vs 1.42 s), which slightly understates the agentic host counts. Chapters 16, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 25 and the back matter are otherwise numerically sound and internally consistent on the checked quantities.
