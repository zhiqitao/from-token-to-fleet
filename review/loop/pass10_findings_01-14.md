# PASS-10 REVIEW — DEEP EDITORIAL FINDINGS ch01-14 ('From Token to Fleet')

Scope: cross-chapter coherence, claim-strength, terminology drift, duplication, use-before-introduction, and whole-book architecture, over design/manuscript/chapter-01..14. Prior numeric passes (1–9) already locked every arithmetic cross-ref; findings below are NEW deep issues, each verified against source. Severity: CRITICAL / MAJOR / MODERATE / MINOR.

No CRITICAL findings (no single defect breaks the book). The top four are MAJOR and are the ones worth acting on.

## MAJOR

### [P10-1] The "Measurement" sections of Ch1, Ch4 and Ch5 are the same three bullets, nearly verbatim (duplication)
**Location:** ch-01 §4 lines 96–98 & closing 100; ch-04 §4 lines 182–186 & closing 188; ch-05 §4 lines 115–119 & closing 121; echoed in ch-06 §4 (90–95) and ch-07 §4 item 2 (101).
**Problem:** Three chapters repeat the *identical* measurement triple with the same phrasing:
- "Run the model's *own tokenizer* … the '4 chars ≈ 1 token' heuristic is for estimation only" (ch01:96 / ch04:182 / ch05:115);
- "Measure both legs of the request … because they land on different bottlenecks — input on memory/prefill, output on decode" (ch01:97 / ch04:184 / ch05:117);
- "Log the distribution of context lengths, not just the mean … averages 9.2K … has a very different KV cache and latency profile" (ch01:98 / ch04:186 / ch05:119);
and the *same sentence* closes each: "This/These measurement habit(s) is/are the token-layer answer to the book's recurring question, 'what would I actually measure here?'" (ch01:100 / ch04:188 / ch05:121), which is again echoed at ch06:95 and ch07:103.
**Why it matters:** The book's own stated template (book-architecture §4) makes "Measurement" a per-chapter section a reader *should* be able to use chapter-independently. As written, a reader who opens Ch4 or Ch5 §4 gets a carbon copy of Ch1's §4, then again the same text in Ch6/Ch7 §4. This is exactly the "same paragraph-level content restated verbatim in 2+ chapters" the review targets, and it is the single largest repetition in ch01–14.
**Recommended change:** Keep the *tokenizer/token-count* habit only in Ch1 (where the unit is taught); in Ch4/Ch5/Ch6/Ch7 §4, replace the identical bullets with a one-line cross-reference ("the token-count habits of Ch1 §4 apply") and write the §4 content specific to *that* chapter's dimension (six-dimension quantification for Ch4, selection surfaces for Ch5, metric-hierarchy instrumentation for Ch6, KV/residency measurement for Ch7).

### [P10-2] design/book-architecture.md's evidence taxonomy contradicts the binding taxonomy (and manuscript practice)
**Location:** design/book-architecture.md §5 (lines ~95–110) and §14 (line ~248) vs the binding taxonomy in render/latex/backmatter/sources.tex (Axis 1/Axis 2) and the per-chapter labels.
**Problem:** book-architecture.md defines a *different* two-axis system than the one the book actually publishes:
- §5 lists the source axis as `[1P] / [2°] / [VERIFY]` and the claim-type axis as `FACT / DERIVED / HYPOTHESIS`, and states "**[VERIFY] is a status**."
- §14 lists epistemic status as the five categories `FACT / MEASURED / DERIVED / ILLUSTRATIVE / HYPOTHESIS` — i.e. it treats **ILLUSTRATIVE as a *status*** — and includes **MEASURED**, a status the book nowhere uses.
- The actual binding taxonomy (backmatter sources.tex, which is what ships in the PDF) and the manuscript practice are: provenance `[1P]/[canonical scenario]/[2°]/[LAB]/[ILLUSTRATIVE]` × status `[FACT]/[DERIVED]/[ASSUMPTION]/[HYPOTHESIS]/[UNRESOLVED]` — where **ILLUSTRATIVE is a provenance** and `[ASSUMPTION]`/`[UNRESOLVED]` are statuses (used in-text as `[ILLUSTRATIVE][ASSUMPTION]`, ch04 Table 4-3) that the architecture doc's §5/§14 list of statuses omits entirely.
**Why it matters:** book-architecture.md §10 declares itself "the authoritative architecture … chapters grow incrementally against this frame." A chapter author following §5/§14 would label `ILLUSTRATIVE` as a status and use `[VERIFY]` and `MEASURED` — none of which appear in the shipping taxonomy, and `[VERIFY]` is explicitly *not* a provenance in the published axis. This is a live taxonomy drift that will propagate mislabels into every future chapter and re-introduces the "[VERIFY]/MEASURED" categories the frozen taxonomy deliberately removed.
**Recommended change:** Rewrite book-architecture §5 and §14 to mirror the sources.tex axes exactly: provenance `[1P]/[canonical scenario]/[2°]/[LAB]/[ILLUSTRATIVE]`; status `[FACT]/[DERIVED]/[ASSUMPTION]/[HYPOTHESIS]/[UNRESOLVED]`; state that `ILLUSTRATIVE` is provenance, `[LAB]` denotes home-lab (non-reference), and `[VERIFY]`/`MEASURED` are retired.

### [P10-3] book-architecture §14 canonical numbers are stale vs the frozen canonical-workload.yaml it governs
**Location:** design/book-architecture.md §14 line ~244 vs design/canonical-workload.yaml (lines 72–101) and ch-07 §4.
**Problem:** The authoritative architecture doc states "per-request KV ≈ **23.8 GB** at 9,500 tokens" and "FP8 KV ≈ **1.3 MB/token**." The frozen canonical set (canonical-workload.yaml, declared "single source of truth" by this very §14) and every chapter use **24.9 GB** at 9.5K (9,500 × 2.62 MB = 24.9 GB) and **1.42 MB/token** (vLLM-measured ~54% of BF16 → 13.4 GB). The "23.8 GB" comes from multiplying 9,500 × 2.5 MB (the MiB shorthand via 2.5 MiB = 2.5 MB), and "1.3 MB" is the *naive byte-halving* that canonical-workload.yaml and ch-07:105 explicitly warn to *not* quote as the operating FP8 number.
**Why it matters:** This is the document that runs the whole book's arithmetic (its own §244 line says "any chapter that needs a quantitative claim derives it from that set; fix the chapter, never the canonical set"). The canonical set's own summary page is now the only place quoting 23.8 GB and 1.3 MB/token, so a chapter author or reviewer reconciling "single source of truth" numbers finds the source doc disagreeing with itself by ~1 GB and by the FP8-vs-8-bit distinction the book worked hard to lock (ch-07 §4 item 4).
**Recommended change:** In book-architecture §14, change to "per-request KV ≈ **24.9 GB** at 9,500 tokens" and "FP8 KV ≈ **1.42 MB/token** (≈54% of BF16 → ~13.4 GB); the naive byte-halving ~1.31 MB/token is the lower bound, not the shipped figure." (The same line's "C ≈ 18 baseline / 16 at T=2 / 14 at T=4" uses undefined "T" and should be aligned to the yaml's C=18/33.)

### [P10-4] The canonical ~$15,000 monthly budget is never reconciled with the book's own derived running cost (whole-book economics coherence)
**Location:** ch-04 canonical box (line 83, "Monthly budget ~$15,000 (illustrative ceiling)"); ch-04 §6 cost-per-request (line 173, ~$0.012/request); ch-04 §6 fleet note (line 175, ~5 avg / ~20 peak hosts) and §8 mini-case (line 233, "~24 hosts … roughly $140K–$350K/month"); carried into ch-12 §8 (line 68, "economic ceiling is $15,000 monthly operating budget").
**Problem:** The canonical box pins the economic ceiling at **$15,000/month**, but the book's own arithmetic costs the workload far more on every basis it uses:
- per-request token basis: ~$0.012 × ~26M requests/month (10 rps × 86,400 × 30) ≈ **~$300K/month**;
- host-infrastructure basis: ~$20/hr × 730 hr ≈ **$14.6K/month for one host**, and the book itself concludes the workload needs ~5 avg / ~20–24 peak hosts → **$73K–$350K/month** (ch-04:175, 233; ch-05/08 prefill sizing ~5 hosts).
So $15,000 is consistent only with a *single* host, the very configuration the book concludes is throughput-insufficient (~5 hosts required). The $15K ceiling is then reused in ch-12 as the "economic ceiling" that every candidate — including the "baseline" — violates, with no note that the canonical budget and the canonical fleet requirement are mutually incompatible.
**Why it matters:** Economics is one of the six workload dimensions and the "monthly budget" sits in the canonical single-reference-set box that every chapter disciplines on. As it stands the book's central economic constraint (≈$15K) is 5–20× below its own derived cost for the workload it defines, and the two cost bases (per-token pricing vs infra per-host) are never reconciled to one budget. This undercuts the TCO thread (Ch16) that the whole arc depends on.
**Recommended change:** Either raise the canonical budget to match the ~5-host floor (≈$75K/month on-demand) with an explicit note, or relabel the $15,000 as "monthly budget *for a single serving host*" and state separately that meeting the canonical arrival rate requires ~5–20 hosts (≈$75K–$350K/month), so the budget is a per-host ceiling, not the workload ceiling. Reconcile the per-request-token and per-host cost bases (say which drives TCO in Ch16) rather than leaving two unreconciled bases.

## MODERATE

### [P10-5] Ch12 uses unverified prefix-cache amortization numbers (30%/50%) as established, then disclaims them as hypothesis in its own §7 (claim-strength)
**Location:** ch-12 §3 candidate (a) line 16 ("reducing effective prefill FLOPs by ~30% at 10 rps average and ~50% at peak 40 rps"); Table 12-1 line 83 ("~28K w/ prefix cache", i.e. 19.8K ÷ 0.7); §8 (lines 72) vs the same chapter §7 line 59 ("The assumed 30–50% prefill amortization rests on the premise … Empirical measurement … is needed to confirm these numbers. (a hypothesis awaiting verification)").
**Problem:** The candidate evaluation and the summary table assert a specific 30%/50% prefill-amortization and a ~28K tok/s consequence as though measured, while §7 explicitly labels the 30–50% range an unverified hypothesis. The table and candidate (a) therefore present a hypothesis as an established result in the exact place (candidate screening) where the book's own §3 thesis says numbers must be defensible.
**Why it matters:** This is a claim-strength leak in the architectural-decision machinery: a reader using Ch12 to pick among candidates would bank the ~28K figure, unaware it rests on an unmeasured hit-rate premise that the same chapter flags as unknown. It also contradicts the book's own "do not present an estimate as fact" rule.
**Recommended change:** In §3/§8 and Table 12-1, label the 30%/50% and the "~28K w/ prefix cache" cell as "[HYPOTHESIS] — pending the prefix-overlap measurement flagged in §7," and remove the precise 28K from the constraint-satisfaction verdict column (or replace with "conditional on measured prefix hit rate").

### [P10-6] Ch9 vs Ch10 state the same DP gradient all-reduce at two different sizes (140 GB vs 280 GB)
**Location:** ch-09 §3 (line 54: "In data-parallel training the gradient tensor has the same size, so every all-reduce moves a gradient tensor of M = 140 GB per rank"); ch-09 §5 common mistake (line 96, ~56 s/step at 2.5 GB/s ⇒ 140 GB); vs ch-10 §3 DP (line 96: "grad bytes = … × 4 B ≈ 280 GB (FP32 gradient)", line 99: 280/25 ≈ 11 s and 280/900 ≈ 0.31 s).
**Problem:** For the *same* "70B data-parallel gradient all-reduce," Ch9 uses **140 GB** (an FP16 gradient) and Ch10 uses **280 GB** (an FP32 gradient). Neither chapter notes that the difference is the gradient precision, so a reader who cross-checks the per-step all-reduce size/time finds two different quantities for the same operation. (Prior pass fixed Ch10's *labeling* as FP32 but did not reconcile Ch9's 140 GB assumption against Ch10's 280 GB.)
**Why it matters:** Communication time is Ch9's whole subject and Ch10's DP worked example pins the gradient-sync cost as the scaling limiter. With two unlabeled gradient sizes (and thus ≈2× different sync times), the book's bandwidth argument is not internally reproducible across the two chapters.
**Recommended change:** State the gradient dType explicitly in both chapters (Ch9: "FP16 gradient, 140 GB" vs Ch10: "FP32 gradient, 280 GB"), or pick one (e.g. FP16/mixed precision, the common deployed case) and use it in both, with the other given as a variant.

### [P10-7] RAG is used 48× across ch01–14 and never expanded; "token layer" is reused as an unnamed abstraction with a copy artifact
**Location:** RAG first appears in ch-03 (used 3×), then 6× (ch04), 20× (ch05), 3× (ch06), 2× (ch07/08), 8× (ch11), 2× (ch12), 1× (ch13/14) — 48 uses in ch01–14, none spelling out "Retrieval-Augmented Generation." It is only expanded in back matter / Ch26 Pattern 11 (book-architecture §8). "the token layer" appears at ch01:123, ch02:160, ch04:225, ch07:171 as if it were a defined layer, though the book never defines a "token layer"; and ch04:225 reads "From the token layer alone (as we did in Ch. 4)" — a copy artifact from ch01:123 ("(which we worked through in this chapter)") that self-references Ch4's own mini-case instead of the actual token chapter (Ch1).
**Why it matters:** The target audience is an architect learning from scratch; RAG is the *name of the whole canonical system* and appears at the top of Ch3's trend section before it is ever defined. And the repeated "token layer" phrase signals a leftover abstraction from an earlier framing that no longer exists in the book's stated layers (token/model/workload/system/architecture/fleet).
**Recommended change:** Expand "RAG (retrieval-augmented generation)" at first use (Ch1 mini-case or Ch4, where it becomes the workload name); and either define "the token layer" once (Ch1 §1) or replace the phrase in ch02:160 / ch04:225 / ch07:171 with concrete wording ("from the unit-and-token work of Ch1"); fix ch04:225's "as we did in Ch. 4" to point at Ch1.

### [P10-8] The KV-per-token formula and the MoE "compute-sparsity ≠ memory-sparsity" lesson are each taught three times (Ch1, Ch3, Ch7)
**Location:** Full KV formula + order-of-magnitude figures at ch-01 §1 lines 41–47 (§"KV cache as a concept", including ~256 KB / ~1.3 MB per token); the MoE "compute sparsity vs memory sparsity" insight at ch01:47, ch-03 §3 lines 57–76 (and boxed rule 76–81), ch-07 §3 lines 30–71 (and Fig 7.1, line 75).
**Problem:** Ch1 — the "concept" chapter whose Architect's Question is about the *token unit* — already states the complete per-token KV formula (line 44), the 7B/70B per-token figures, and the MoE-shrinks-compute-not-memory lesson (line 47), conceding "we only need the concept here; the mechanism inventory is Chapter 3's, the arithmetic Chapter 7's." Yet Ch3 §3 and Ch7 §3 then re-derive the same formula and re-state the same sparsity distinction (Ch3's "Key takeaway" 76, Ch7's "parameter count does not determine KV size" 147).
**Why it matters:** This is a "concept used/stated before it is properly introduced" problem plus duplication: the hardest KV insight of the book (MoE ≠ memory-sparsity) and its central formula surface at full strength in Ch1, then again in Ch3 and Ch7. A reader is told the full formula before the model chapter (Ch3) or the memory chapter (Ch7) has earned it, and the scaffolding note ("we only need the concept here") is contradicted by the completeness of what Ch1 already asserts.
**Recommended change:** In Ch1 §1, keep only the *qualitative* KV concept (that decode reuses stored K/V and it grows with length) and the single per-token order-of-magnitude; move the full formula and the MoE-sparsity lesson entirely to Ch3 §3 / Ch7 §3, where they belong, so each is taught once at the right rung of the spine.

## MINOR

### [P10-9] Ch6 uses Ch8's roofline vocabulary before Ch8 defines it
**Location:** ch-06 §3 line 66 ("its low arithmetic intensity (~1.0 FLOP/byte, Ch 8), far below the ~295 ridge") and §Resource (it leans on "ridge"/"FLOP/byte").
**Problem:** "Arithmetic intensity," "ridge," and "FLOP/byte" are formally introduced in Ch8 (Concept §1, lines 9–21, and the ridge figure 8.1/8.2), which is *after* Ch6. Ch6 cross-references Ch8, so it is not a hard error, but the reader encounters the central roofline concept (and the concrete 295 ridge number) in Ch6 before the chapter that teaches it.
**Why it matters:** Roofline is the diagnostic backbone of the compute/bandwidth-bound claim; meeting its terminology and numbers in Ch6, before Ch8 constructs them, is a forward-reference cost in an otherwise carefully-sequenced book.
**Recommended change:** Either move the ridge/intensity-definitional one-liner to Ch6 or demote Ch6's use to "the arithmetic-intensity / roofline distinction developed in Ch8" without quoting the 295 ridge before Ch8.

### [P10-10] [S1]–[S6] bibliographic keys are used as though they belong to the provenance axis
**Location:** ch-06 (7 uses), ch-07, ch-11 (14 uses), ch-13, ch-15, ch-17, ch-22, ch-25, ch-27 — e.g. ch-11 "[S1][1P]", "[S2][1P]", "[S4][1P]" (lines 11–17, 107–110); ch-06 "[S4][1P]", "[S3][1P]", "[S6][1P]" (lines 70, 109–118).
**Problem:** The provenance axis is `[1P]/[2°]/[canonical scenario]/[LAB]/[ILLUSTRATIVE]`. The `[S#]` keys are a *bibliography* index (defined only in render/latex/backmatter/references.tex, "S1..S6"), but chapters combine them as if they were provenance items ("[S4][1P]"), and the compound label omits the epistemic-status slot (sources.tex allows one axis to be implied, but here both a bibliographic index and a provenance appear while status is unstated).
**Why it matters:** It mixes two distinct label systems (bibliography key + provenance), and leaves the status axis unspecified for the biggest design claims (e.g. MoE 6B/125B active fraction claims in ch03 use `[1P: HF Qwen/…]` instead — an inconsistent citation style). A reader reconciling labels finds `[S1][1P]` (ch11) vs `[1P: HF Qwen/…]` (ch03) for the same kind of first-party claim.
**Recommended change:** Standardize: use `[S#]` purely as the citation index and attach it to an explicit provenance+status pair (e.g. "`[S1][1P][FACT]`"), or drop `[S#]` in favor of the `[1P: <source>]` form used in ch03, so one convention is used throughout.

### [P10-11] Ch12 names the single 8×H100 "baseline" candidate while its own table rejects it on throughput
**Location:** ch-12 §3 candidate (a) (lines 16 & 24 "baseline"), Table 12-1 row (a) (line 83, "✗ throughput: needs ~5 hosts"), §8 (line 72); contrasted with the clean split elsewhere: ch02:69, ch11:148, ch13:41 which explicitly mark the single host "latency-feasible but not capacity-sufficient."
**Problem:** Ch2/Ch11/Ch13 carefully distinguish per-request latency feasibility from aggregate capacity, and ch11 scope note (148) and ch13 §3 (41) both insist the single host cannot carry the arrival rate. Ch12, by calling candidate (a) "the baseline" then marking its own table "needs ~5 hosts," names a configuration that cannot serve the workload as the chapter's baseline candidate — without the per-request-vs-capacity framing its siblings use.
**Why it matters:** Whole-book architecture coherence: the "single 8×H100" appears throughout the early book as *the* canonical serving baseline (ch2/4/7/8/11), and Ch12's candidate set should either frame it with the same latency-vs-capacity caveat or name the ~5-host replica as the baseline.
**Recommended change:** Add the per-request-latency-vs-aggregate-capacity caveat to Ch12 candidate (a) and to its "baseline" label (matching ch11:148 / ch13:41), or relabel the baseline as "the single-host *per-request-*baseline, which the throughput gate rejects."

### [P10-12] Ch7 Fig 7.1 caption calls an illustration a "proof"
**Location:** ch-07 line 75 ("The visual proof of the 8× saving"), and same usage at ch-07 Fig 7.2 caption (line 147).
**Problem:** The 8× GQA-vs-MHA reduction is a *derivation* (0.33 vs 2.62 MB/token). A figure that illustrates it is an illustration, not a proof; "proof" over-claims the epistemic status of a schematic diagram, against the book's own rule that only [FACT]/[DERIVED] claims carry proof-like weight.
**Why it matters:** Minor claim-strength wording that lets a schematic be read as evidence.
**Recommended change:** "The figure illustrates the 8× reduction from sharing K/V across a group of query heads" (and likewise in Fig 7.2's caption).
