## FINDINGS

### 1. [MODERATE] Fig A.1 (appendix, p.268) — grouped horizontal BAR chart on a LOG
###     x-axis under-encodes the very ratio the figure exists to show

- Severity: MODERATE
- Location: `design/manuscript/chapter-27/figures/fig-27-2701.png` (rendered pp.287),
  generated by `render/fig_2701.py` (`ax.set_xscale('log')`, `ax.set_xlim(1, 9000)`),
  caption "Frontier MoE sparsity in 2026 … the anchor for the Ch10 expert-parallelism
  serving calculus."
- Problem: The figure encodes total-vs-active parameters as two horizontal bars per
  model on a LOG x-axis. Bar length is therefore proportional to log(value), not value,
  so a total→active ratio of ~20–27× (e.g. Kimi 2,800B vs 104B; GLM 320B vs 18B)
  renders as a fixed log-distance rather than a visually-obvious 20–27× length gap.
  A reader comparing bar lengths under-perceives the extreme sparsity that is the
  figure's entire point ("single-digit active fraction"). Additionally, the inline
  "% of total" labels are placed at `active_b[i]*1.15` (a multiplicative offset), which
  on a log axis distances them from the bar end and (for the worst cases) can sit over
  or beside the total bar; and the Kimi total bar (2,800 B) extends well beyond the
  labelled 10³ major tick (xlim 1→9000), so its full length is off the labelled scale.
- Why it matters: This is a section-24 figure-causality failure — "could the reader
  infer something false even though every label is technically correct?" The labels say
  "3.7%/4.6%/5.6%/4.8%", but the visual geometry (log–log bar lengths) understates the
  sparsity ratio, which is the headline architectural message the appendix uses to
  motivate the Ch10 expert-parallelism serving calculus.
- Recommended change: Re-encode as (a) a LINEAR x-axis horizontal bar chart (bar length
  then directly encodes the total/active ratio), or (b) a two-axis dot/paired-point or
  scatter of total vs active, with the ratio called out. If the log axis is retained
  deliberately because the totals span ~1–3,000 B, then plot ACTIVE FRACTION as the
  primary quantity (e.g. a bar of active/total % per model, which is the message) and
  keep total/active in a secondary table, rather than using log bars whose length
  visually suppress the ratio.

### 2. [MODERATE] Ch16 self-hosted per-token cost (~$0.60/1M) vs Ch18 per-1M model
###     price table ($2.50/1M for the identical 70B FP16 / 8×H100 deployment) — 4.2×
###     cross-chapter mismatch with no reconciliation

- Severity: MODERATE
- Location: Ch16 §16.4.2/§16.4 Step 2 (self-hosted per request = $148K ÷ 26M req ≈
  $0.0057/req ⇒ $5.7/1K req ⇒ **≈$0.60 per 1M tokens**) vs Ch18 §3.2 Model-cost table
  ("70B dense FP16 (8×H100 capital amortized): **$2.50 /1M**, basis 'capital + energy'",
  used in Ch18 §3.3 routing arithmetic and again as the §3.5 cost-fraction target
  "< $2.50/1M").
- Problem: Both figures price the SAME deployment — 70B dense FP16 self-hosted on an
  8×H100 host on a "capital + energy" (capex+opex) basis — yet differ by ≈4.2×
  ($0.60 vs $2.50 per 1M tokens). Verified independently: ch16 fleet serves 26M req/mo ×
  9,500 tokens = ~2.47×10¹¹ tokens/mo; $148K ÷ 2.47×10¹¹ = $5.99×10⁻⁷/token = $0.60/1M
  tokens. No bridge or scope note reconciles the two in either chapter.
- Why it matters: Section 5/21 of the review prompt — "a number that is individually
  correct but inconsistent with another chapter is still a book-level defect." The
  reader cannot tell the true FP16 per-token cost, which is the linchpin of Ch18's
  mixed-fleet routing economics ($8.31/$1.90/$2.38 → $12.59/hr vs $23.75/hr, and the
  47% savings claim). If the real self-hosted cost is $0.60/1M, the single-FP16
  baseline in Ch18 ($23.75/hr at $2.50/1M) is over-stated by ~4×, materially weakening
  the mixed-fleet-savings conclusion.
- Recommended change: Reconcile the two bases explicitly — either (a) raise Ch16's
  derived per-token number or lower Ch18's table entry so they agree on one number, or
  (b) scope them explicitly (e.g. Ch18 table is a per-model per-1M PRICE observation on
  a single host at its nominal throughput, while Ch16 is the fleet-amortized cost on the
  peak-provisioned fleet), and cross-reference each to the other so a reader can see the
  basis difference. At minimum, add a one-line note in Ch18 §3.2 distinguishing its
  per-1M table price from the Ch16 TCO-derived per-token cost and noting the ~4× gap is
  a utilization/assumption difference, not a contradiction.

### 3. [MODERATE] Ch16 prices the fleet at the full-utilization host count (≈20) while
###     the book's own sizing rule targets 70% utilization (⇒ ≈28 hosts); TCO shows no
###     economic sensitivity to that provisioning policy

- Severity: MODERATE
- Location: Ch16 §16.4.1/Step 2 ("N_hosts ≈ 20 … provisioned for the 40 rps peak");
  Ch16 Table 16-1 (capacity "~20 hosts", opex "~$148K"); vs Ch17 §4/§5.1/Table 17-1 and
  Ch17 §7 (H = ⌈λ_peak·W/(C·util_target)⌉ with util_target = 0.70 ⇒ **≈28 hosts** for the
  same 40 rps canonical peak, FP16 KV) and Ch16's own aside "~28 at the 70% utilization
  target."
- Problem: Ch16 prices the self-hosted mode on the FULL-utilization fleet (20 hosts,
  $116K capex + $22K opex + $10K staff ≈ $148K/mo, $5.7/1K req) and derives the
  "self-host loses to scale-to-load cloud" conclusion from those figures. But the fleet's
  own sizing rule (Ch17 Table 17-1 and mini-case) sizes at a 70% utilization target →
  28 hosts for the same 40 rps peak. Provisioning the book's own target pushes capex to
  ~1.4× ($163K/mo) and cost/req to ~$8/1K, which materially shifts the Ch16 cost
  comparison against the managed API and scale-to-load cloud. No sensitivity run is
  given for the 70%-utilization fleet.
- Why it matters: Section 33 (economic reasoning) requires checking utilization
  assumptions and ensuring cost conclusions follow from explicit assumptions. A reader
  applying the book's own 70% sizing rule gets a ~40% higher self-host cost than the
  number on which the Ch16 "self-host loses to cloud" verdict rests.
- Recommended change: Either price the self-hosted mode at the 70%-utilization fleet
  (≈28 hosts) to match Ch17's sizing rule, or add one explicit sentence of economic
  sensitivity: "if the fleet is provisioned at the 70% utilization target (≈28 hosts),
  self-hosted cost rises to ≈$207K/mo (≈$8/1K), which [strengthens/weakens] the conclusion
  vs scale-to-load cloud." State which provisioning policy the TCO result assumes.
