**Source:** `from-token-to-fleet-v20260913(20261101-151711).pdf`

**Source scope:** 441 US-Letter PDF pages, 26 numbered chapters, 57 numbered figures, Preface, Appendix A, Glossary, References, Sources/Method/Reproducibility, Architecture Worksheet.  
**Overall verdict:** **HOLD / not yet publishing-ready**, pending the critical reconciliation and production fixes below.  
**Review level:** Automated whole-document structural preflight; systematic page-by-page text/figure-location inventory; visual survey of all 57 figures; high-resolution inspection of selected figures, tables and layout; independent recomputation of the numerical examples explicitly cited below; source spot-check of the DeepSeek service-status claim against the primary vendor change log. ## Executive assessment

The manuscript now has a well-articulated intellectual spine: architect a real AI service by carrying workload assumptions into memory, compute, communication, serving, fleet, cost, validation and architecture decision records. Its strongest improvements are scope/epistemic disclaimers, careful differentiation of analytic capacity bounds from measured SLO-goodput, and explicit treatment of different token/memory geometries. The diagrams generally teach mechanisms rather than decorate the page. The 57 figures are present and the observed raster graphics have effective resolutions of about **350–600 dpi**; no detected glyph bounding box extends beyond the physical PDF page. The PDF has **792 link annotations**, with no invalid internal page destinations detected, and **38 bookmarks**.

The remaining publication risk is not primarily elementary mistakes. It is the harder problem of **assumptions migrating between chapters**: the same host-count number is described using varying conventions (efficiency, headroom, failover, FLOP counting); a provenance tag sometimes becomes a claim of validation; a current-status update is added without corresponding revision history; and a few examples use carefully caveated but materially misleading simplifying labels.

**Severity scale:** P0 = demonstrably false or dangerous load-bearing conclusion; P1 = release-blocking substantive reconciliation/traceability/readability defect; P2 = should fix before first print/professional edition; P3 = optional polish. **No P0 claim was proven in this pass.** The P1 items still justify withholding release.

## Release-blocking and substantive findings

### P1-01 — Efficiency, utilization and N−1 failover are not consistently composed

**Locations:** printed pp. 237–238 (Chapter 16), 261–265 (Chapter 17), 313–315 (Chapter 20); Fig. 17.1 (p. 255).

The book calculates 30 hosts at a 70% max-operating-utilization policy with one reserve: 29 working hosts × (17 KV slots / 8.6 sec) × 0.70 = **40.12 req/s**. Elsewhere the book introduces an independent-looking throughput-efficiency factor **rho ≈ 0.95**; Chapter 20 makes that derate explicit.

If the two percentages are independent, then after losing a host the sustained capacity is

`29 × 17 / 8.6 × 0.70 × 0.95 ≈ 38.12 req/s`, **not 40 req/s**.

The internally consistent KV-only formula would be `N_total = ceil(40 × 8.6 / (17 × 0.70 × 0.95)) + 1 = 32` (31 working + 1 reserve). The p. 237 **30-host** row would then understate the illustrative deployment by two hosts. Cost at the same capex/energy/staff assumptions rises from **$218,309.90/month ($34.99/1K requests)** to approximately **$232,197.23/month ($37.21/1K)**. The independent case-(ii) full-square prefill screen, when *also* derated by 95% and 70%, would require approximately **34 total** rather than 33, under the same no-double-counting assumption.

**Important qualification:** If rho was intended to be entirely included inside the 70% utilization safety factor, applying it again would double-count loss. In that case, explicitly DEFINE `u` as the *net* admission/throughput derate and remove rho from that formula; do not introduce rho later as a separate efficiency multiplier. The present text does not establish that convention robustly enough.

**Fix:** Add one authoritative policy table that defines `C`, `W_KV`, `u`, `rho`, and `N_spare`, states which factors are independent, and propagates the same formula consistently through Ch. 16, Ch. 17, Ch. 20, TCO, and figures. Re-run both KV-only and full-square-prefill counts. The result is still an **analytical lower-bound screen, not a measured SLO-goodput guarantee**.

**Acceptance test:** Recompute all 21/22/29/30/32/33/34 host counts from one parameterized script; test u={1.0, 0.7}, rho={1.0, 0.95}, spare={0,1}, and each FLOP-counting case. All chapter mentions and figure labels must match the selected policy.

### P1-02 — Figure 17.1 claims failover clears a burst without accounting for stated efficiency

**Location:** printed p. 255 / PDF p. 275, Fig. 17.1.

The graph claims a four-host fleet has N−1 residual capacity ~6.0 req/s, above a ~5.5 req/s burst. Its own following text allows throughput-efficiency factors around 0.85–0.98. Three surviving hosts sustain only `3 × ~2.0 × rho`. At rho=0.85 the capacity is about **5.1 req/s**, below the plotted burst; the pass/fail crossover is rho≈0.917. At rho=0.95 the margin is small; for the exact C/W rate it is about 5.63 req/s.

**Fix:** Label the plot explicitly **ideal rho=1, analytical service ceiling, no SLO claim**, or use a five-host example and calculate a robust lower bound with rho and a measured per-host capacity. Do not call the depicted 0.5 req/s of nominal capacity a robust failover margin.

### P1-03 — The source taxonomy is occasionally used to certify a later epistemic stage

**Locations:** printed p. 57, pp. 411–413, and worked dialogue on p. 335.

The source taxonomy rightly insists on three orthogonal axes: provenance (`[1P]`, `[LAB]`, `[2°]`, `[ILLUSTRATIVE]`), epistemic status (`[FACT]`, `[DERIVED]`, `[ASSUMPTION]`, `[HYPOTHESIS]` etc.), and measurement stage (`[ANALYTICAL]`, `[MODELED]`, `[MEASURED]`). Yet the p. 57 assumption-change passage describes hypotheses as tagged **`[HYPOTHESIS] or [ILLUSTRATIVE]`** (not the same axis) and validated results as tagged **`[LAB] or [2°]`** (both source origins, neither alone demonstrates achieved SLO-goodput). On printed p. 335 an invented, explicitly illustrative customer dialogue is marked **`[2°]`**, without an identified secondary source.

**Fix:** State `provenance × epistemic status × measurement stage` in the change-log passage. An illustrative hypothesized result might be `[ILLUSTRATIVE][HYPOTHESIS][UNMEASURED]`; a measured benchmark with matching hardware and recorded trace might be `[LAB][FACT][MEASURED]`, with explicit acceptance criteria. Correct the illustrative dialogue to suitable fictional/scenario provenance (or identify a genuine secondary source). A local capability probe is **not** a production workload validation, as the Sources chapter already observes.

**Acceptance test:** Search all evidence-label occurrences and reject automatic inference `LAB → MEASURED`, `2° → validated`, or `ILLUSTRATIVE → HYPOTHESIS`. Keep a separate proof-of-SLO gate.

### P1-05 — Network transfer-time example confuses latency, bulk transfer duration, and bandwidth

**Location:** printed p. 265 (Ch. 17, §17.4.4).

The example sets `D=5 MB`, `t_net=20 ms` (called a “typical rack latency”), then calculates `D/t_net=250 MB/s ≈2 Gbps` as a **per-request burst**. This is not generally a line-rate burst or an inherent property of a 10-GbE link: if 20 ms is *fixed network/service latency*, data can transmit at a much higher instantaneous rate during a shorter interval; if 20 ms is total transfer completion time, it includes transfer and queuing, and cannot independently define the bandwidth ceiling. A 20-ms RTT is also not a representative physical intra-rack fabric latency on modern low-latency infrastructure (see AWS low-latency placement and NVIDIA RoCE materials); it may be a *service-level retrieval round trip*.

**Fix:** Define `t_fixed` (network RTT + serialization independent overhead), `B_effective` (measured achievable per-flow throughput), and `t_transfer = t_fixed + D/B_effective + t_queue`, and compare **aggregate offered byte rate** `phi × lambda × D` against the relevant shared link/bisection budget. If a 20-ms wall-clock retrieval operation is intended, call it that and do not label it rack network latency. Clarify that `D / observed duration` is an *effective per-request completion goodput*, not necessarily the instantaneous link burst. The 50-MB/20-ms overload scenario is then clear and physically grounded.

### P2-01 — Table 19-1 knowingly displays a different rounding from its own formula

**Location:** printed p. 303 / PDF p. 323; nearby pp. 293–294.

Table 19-1 prints **W(T=3) ≈ 13.8 s**, but its note calculates `1.08 + 0.025×495 + 3×0.133 = 13.854 s`, which correctly rounds to **13.9 s**, not 13.8. The note explicitly acknowledges the difference and preserves the older Ch. 17 value. That is a publishing defect, even though its impact on host count is small.

**Fix:** Use 13.85 s (or rounded 13.9 s) consistently in Ch. 17, Ch. 19 and downstream sizing; alternatively change an explicitly declared input so 13.8 derives correctly. Rerun all adjacent table cells. Also rename the Table 19-1 row currently labeled “Incremental orchestration” when it includes the full 8.6-s base service time; a name such as “Base + orchestration subtotal” is less confusing.

### P2-02 — Architecture recommendation overreaches the demonstrated model

**Location:** printed p. 333 (end of Ch. 22 mini-case).

The case says the fact that prefill is compute-bound and decode bandwidth-bound **“makes clear that a homogeneous design will be suboptimal for either bottleneck.”** That does not follow without quantitative resource scheduling, transfer overhead, model residency, utilization and SLO measurements. A homogeneous server can be economically or operationally optimal despite differing phase bottlenecks. The book itself teaches that P/D disaggregation is a candidate that wins only when its specialization benefit exceeds KV-transfer and orchestration costs.

**Fix:** Change to “makes P/D disaggregation a candidate worth testing; benchmarking and TCO determine whether it wins.” Cross-link Fig. 11.3.

### P2-03 — Availability/geo-disaster recovery remains out of the costed canonical architecture

**Locations:** printed pp. 55–56, 233–240, 254–263, Architecture Worksheet p. 421.

The canonical scenario requires multi-region active-active and 99.9% availability, while the costed fleet is single-region and N−1 host-failure only. The manuscript admits this, so it is not a hidden fabrication; nevertheless it is incomplete as a canonical solution example. A reader needs an explicit “candidate does not meet all requirements” statement at the final recommendation, not only an earlier disclaimer.

**Fix:** End the canonical example with a requirement-coverage matrix: functional/quality, peak load, TTFT/TPOT, regional failure, data locality, restore/recovery, error budget, and TCO. Mark `NOT PRICED / NOT VALIDATED` for multi-region. Optionally give a separate fully bounded regional-resilience sensitivity, without pretending it is empirically validated.

### P2-04 — The high-signal 2026 vendor appendix risks becoming a moving target

**Locations:** Appendix A, especially pp. 377–402.

The appendix responsibly separates historical snapshots from dated later notes, but repeatedly revisiting volatile products inside the same immutable base edition increases the chance that 2026 service status, released source, and measured support drift apart. Figs A.1 and A.2 are sourced vendor-relative comparisons, not portable operating benchmarks. The newly corrected V4 service-status passage is a case in point.

**Fix:** Freeze the historical comparison as data, maintain one single “as served today” clearly date/versioned box, and give every postcutoff addendum its own revision marker. Require links, version, vendor claim vs independently reproduced status, and date for every volatile row. A high-quality, stable book should not present same-day API availability as an enduring architectural property.

### P2-05 — Package/print production specifications need an explicit release gate

**Locations:** global PDF production, especially color diagrams; copyright/title/metadata.

The PDF is **US Letter (612×792 pt, 8.5×11 in)**. That may be the chosen trim, but it should not be treated as automatically the intended retail paperback trim. Some figures rely on red/green contrast; grayscale or budget monochrome printing needs proofing. All sampled raster figure assets are high-resolution enough (350–600 effective dpi), and no page-outside text spans were detected. A structural scan cannot replace manual press proof, gutter check, bleed, binding width, barcode/cover wrap verification, or retailer-specific pagination.

**Fix:** Declare intended print trim, color/BW edition, bleed, minimum effective figure font, and paper stock before release. If target trim differs from Letter, reflow typesetting rather than scaling. Inspect all diagrams in grayscale and at physical size. Confirm the separate full-wrap print cover, including spine width and bleed, rather than assuming the vector front cover page itself is a KDP-ready cover file.

### P2-06 — Expand the high-value solved example with a measured-vs-analytical closure

**Locations:** Chs. 14–17, 20, 22, 25.

The analytic 17-slot ceiling, 19/21/22-host prefill screens, price model and 6.24M monthly-request denominator are mostly internally coherent. But the book does not provide a measured SLO-goodput datapoint on the specified 8×H100 canonical stack. The current disclaimers are technically responsible. Pedagogically, a worked end-state that lands on `UNMEASURED` can frustrate a reader expecting a deployment recommendation.

**Fix:** Add a clearly optional, illustrative *benchmark protocol + result worksheet*, not fabricated measurements. Show exactly which load trace, TTFT/TPOT percentile data, KV allocator usage, rank imbalance, admission rejection, hardware utilization, model ID and backend commit would advance the analytical candidate to a validated one.

### P2-07 — Quantitative tables and figure captions use a prose layout that harms teachability

**Locations:** pp. 54–57 (canonical ledger), Fig. 11.3 (p. 181), Fig. 15.1 (p. 223), Fig. 16.1 (p. 250), Fig. 22.1 (p. 328), Appendix notes.

Printed p. 54 is dominated by a two-column table whose right “value” cell consists of multi-paragraph source and interpretation, making the left half of the page largely blank. Even when no text is clipped, this creates a bad scan path. Figures 11.3, 16.1 and 22.1 have captions approaching full explanatory subsections, repeating disclaimers a reader should find in nearby body prose. A graphic's key insight is lost under evidence qualification.

**Fix:** Use a short table of key numerical inputs (`Field | Value | Unit | Provenance`) followed by a separate “Why these choices / scope” block. Enforce figure captions of ~40–80 words as an editorial target (not a correctness cap): one takeaway; one geometry/source line; one crucial warning. Keep the full derivation, counterfactual, and literature caveats in a numbered paragraph immediately after the figure. Reflow and visually proof the resulting pages.

## C. Findings must distinguish proven defects from conditional concerns and editorial preferences

Apply the following evidence classes *in addition to* severity. A finding can be serious without being proven; an unproven concern must not become an unconditional error statement.

| Evidence class | Required proof | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| `VERIFIED` | Exact source quote + location + reproducible counterexample, failed test, or independent calculation that contradicts the printed claim under the **same stated premises** | Fix, with regression test |
| `CONDITIONAL` | Explicit premise that changes the result (e.g., is `rho` independent of `u`?) plus calculations for both interpretations | Resolve policy/definition first; then recompute dependent text |
| `NOT-YET-VALIDATED` | Requirement or deployment claim for which proof/benchmark/pricing is intentionally absent | Preserve the honest `HOLD` state; do not invent measurement or fail it as an arithmetic defect |
| `PRODUCTION-GATE` | Print, rights, accessibility or packaging evidence absent | Assign owner and acceptance artifact; cannot be closed by prose alone |
| `EDITORIAL` | Concrete readability, navigability or teaching-cost evidence, preferably an actual-size sample | Fix with a targeted layout edit; by itself not necessarily P1 |
| `VERIFY` | Suspicion or apparent source conflict without completed reproduction | Investigate; not a confirmed defect and cannot be used to inflate severity |

**Specific calibration for this report:**

- **P1-01** should be read as a *release-blocking ambiguity in factor policy*, **not** an unconditional proof that 30 hosts is mathematically wrong. `30` follows when the 70% limit is the effective net planning derate and `rho` is not an additional independent loss; `32` follows if `u=0.70` and `rho=0.95` multiply independently. Choose/declare the policy, then test it. Neither number is verified SLO-goodput.
- **P1-02** is likewise a *conditional figure-label/assumption error*: Figure 17.1's approximately 6.0 req/s failover ceiling holds for the idealized `rho=1` case; the conclusion is unsafe if applied to lower `rho` without updating the plot.
- **P1-04** is a *traceable-release/versioning gate*, not evidence the November source claim itself is false.
- **Former P1-06 is classified P2-07** in this revised report. Excessive caption length is a concrete pedagogical defect, but absent proof of unreadable printed content it should not independently block technical sign-off at P1.
- **P2-03** is a *known unresolved hard requirement*. The single-region example may remain pedagogically valuable, provided the final ADR is explicitly **NOT COMMITTABLE** on the multi-region requirement and never presents single-region TCO as a matching full solution.

Do **not** classify a P2/P3 stylistic preference as P1 merely because it was found in a late pass. Do not discard a proven technical P1 merely because repeated reviews are inconvenient.

## D. The single authoritative numeric ledger and executable invariants

Every load-bearing canonical constant should be defined once in machine-readable data, with units, precision, scenario identifier and provenance; tables, text examples, figures, worksheets and TCO outputs should be generated from or mechanically checked against that same ledger. No chapter may silently redefine a factor.

**Minimum canonical controls** (the current manuscript uses these *illustratively*, not as production benchmark observations):

| Symbol / key | Value or status | Interpretation / invariant |
|---|---|---|
| `n_layers`, `d_hidden`, `kv_element_bytes` | 80, 8192, 2 | Full-MHA BF16/FP16 raw KV geometry |
| `tokens_input`, `tokens_output` | 9200, 300 | Canonical token workload |
| `kv_raw_bytes_per_token` | 2,621,440 | Must equal `2 × layers × hidden × bytes` |
| `kv_raw_GB_per_request` | 24.90368 GB | Must use **decimal** GB at 9500 tokens; if MiB/GiB used, convert and label |
| `kv_nominal_budget_GB`, `kv_integer_slots` | 436 GB, 17 | 17 is an *ideal aggregate raw screen*, not guaranteed per-rank residency |
| `W_KV_seconds` | 8.6 in this particular screen | Use only if this equals the defined mean KV-held time, not full E2E latency by default |
| `lambda_peak_req_s` | 40 | Workload target for a specific offer/region scope |
| `u`, `rho` | `0.70`, `0.95` in relevant scenarios | **Must explicitly declare whether independent.** Never multiply both silently |
| `N_spare` | 1 in N−1 scenario | Spare count is capacity/failure policy, not a multiplier |
| `frozen_monthly_requests` | 6,240,000 | Workload calendar must accompany cost-per-1K figures |

Core checks to automate:

1. `kv_raw_bytes_per_token == 2*80*8192*2` and `kv_raw_GB_per_request == kv_raw_bytes_per_token*9500/1e9`.
2. `slots == floor(436/24.90368) == 17`; separately test each rank/partition's actual residency before claiming deployability.
3. For **each explicitly named scenario**, compute `N_working = ceil(lambda_peak*W_KV/(slots*u*rho))` and `N_total = N_working+N_spare`. If `u` already includes the effective `rho`, set `rho=1` *in this formula* and document why. Expect `30` at `(u=.70,rho=1,N_spare=1)` and `32` at `(u=.70,rho=.95,N_spare=1)`.
4. Check the failover residual as `(N_total-N_failed)*slots/W_KV*u*rho` (using the actual surviving **working** capacity model), versus the named burst/peak. Define whether a spare starts cold or participates actively and whether failover requires replacement/spin-up time; `N−1` should not automatically imply zero-latency recovery.
5. Reconcile full-square / causal-triangle / weight-linear FLOP policies separately, with explicit count conventions; no shared floating-point coefficient without a case label.
6. Verify `monthly_requests == 10*8*260*3600/12 == 6_240_000`, and recompute every derived `/request` and `/1K` cost from the scenario's *same* fleet topology, utilization and availability scope.
7. Verify rounding policy (`13.854 → 13.9` at one decimal) plus exact unrounded values used downstream; do not preserve an old rounded table cell for chapter consistency.
8. Fail the build if generated figures/tables disagree with canonical source values beyond a documented display-rounding tolerance, or if numeric values are hard-coded in multiple independent places without a verification search.

The purpose is to make a change in **one** efficiency or token assumption visibly break all dependent tests until the author has updated the complete architecture chain. A self-review that merely reads each chapter independently will not catch this reliably.

## E. Hermes / DeepSeek controlled self-review loop

Operate in two roles, preferably different model configurations or independent contexts. The authoring agent may propose fixes; its own approval is not the final evidence of closure.

**Stage 0 — Freeze and baseline.** Create a git tag/branch, source SHA, PDF SHA, exact compiler/tool versions, generator hashes, a `defects.csv`/JSON ledger and golden numeric test output. Freeze feature/content additions, new vendor-product sections and diagram redesigns unless a verified defect requires them. Allow page-count changes from legitimate reflow, but require a short explanation for large changes.

**Stage 1 — Reproduce before editing.** For each ticket, retrieve the exact PDF passage and its source-of-truth LaTeX or generator. State the violated claim and independently reproduce the disagreement with a test or a small counterexample. If two interpretations are possible, write both out. Mark `VERIFY` instead of editing on intuition.

**Stage 2 — Minimal patch with dependency map.** Correct the *definition or source parameter*, not just a visible downstream sentence. Search the entire manuscript, figure generators, worksheet, appendix addenda, source ledger and TCO tables for affected terms/values. Enumerate impacted pages/figures and reject changes outside that blast radius unless necessary.

**Stage 3 — Machine regression.** Rebuild deterministically and run: numeric/unit/rounding tests, label/provenance lint, cross-reference and broken-link tests, source-key manifest checks, figure-generation tests, and structural PDF preflight. A tool output or diff is the evidence, not the model's statement “looks fixed.”

**Stage 4 — Independent challenge.** Provide the checker with the frozen requirements, rebuilt PDF, numerical ledger and tests, but **not** the fixer's positive self-assessment as the answer to copy. Ask the checker to falsify the corrected claim and inspect its dependency graph. Only a passing independent check closes the ticket. If the checker objects, it must attach the failed test or specific passage, not a broad opinion.

**Stage 5 — Scope-limited visual proof.** Render changed figures/tables at the **intended retail trim**, grayscale and normal color; inspect labels, arrows, collisions, contrast, note alignment, caption/page-break continuity and legend distinctions. Do not repeatedly restyle figures already accepted without a new reproducible complaint.

**Stage 6 — Release-candidate audit.** After all known P0/P1 are closed, perform two independent defect-discovery passes against the *same frozen PDF hash*. Each pass must confirm prior regression tests and may introduce genuinely new issues only with reproducible evidence. All newly found defects reopen the affected release gate with an explicit reason.

## F. Defect ledger format and strict close rule

Maintain a durable defect ID across revisions; the absence of an issue from a later review is **not** proof of closure. Each record must contain:

`id | first_seen_pdf_sha | last_seen_pdf_sha | printed_page | pdf_page | figure/table | quoted_claim | evidence_class | severity | violated_invariant | independent_reproduction | source_files | patch_commit | affected_outputs | tests_before | tests_after | reviewer | status | closure_evidence`.

Valid statuses: `OPEN`, `CONDITIONAL-NEEDS-POLICY`, `VERIFY`, `PATCHED-UNVERIFIED`, `CLOSED-VERIFIED`, `ACCEPTED-WITH-RATIONALE`, `REOPENED`.

**Close only if:** (a) the original failing test no longer fails on the new artifact, (b) the correction is valid under an explicitly recorded premise, (c) its dependent chapters and figures are updated, (d) the independent checker signs off, and (e) the release artifact hash/commit is recorded. Never allow `PATCHED-UNVERIFIED` to be reported as `CLOSED-VERIFIED`.

For a previously found issue absent from the current text, create a **regression ticket**, run the original test and close it with evidence; do not count it as a new defect every time it is rediscovered. If a reviewer raises the same point without new evidence, append the reference to the existing ticket instead of multiplying the defect count.
