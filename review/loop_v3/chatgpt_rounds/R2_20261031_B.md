## 1. Decision

**Verdict: HOLD final publication; technically advanced release candidate, but not an approved print master.**

The manuscript's architecture is unusually coherent: the token → workload → memory/compute/communication → serving → architecture/TCO → fleet → professional decision loop is traceable. Most importantly, the book distinguishes *modeled capacity*, *hard KV residency*, *uncontended service time*, *offered-load feasibility*, and *SLO-qualified measured goodput*. Several of the most obvious errors in inference-system writing (treating active MoE parameters as resident parameters, treating FP8 latency improvement as an FP8 footprint ratio, or using 1-second TTFT as full request service time) are explicitly avoided.

I **did not substantiate a new catastrophic error in the 70B/80-layer canonical KV-cache arithmetic, the core Chapter 8 prefill throughput approximation, or the headline Chapter 16/17 host-count divisions**. That is a meaningful positive finding, but not an exhaustive proof that every equation in the book is correct.

The remaining problems are concentrated in (a) rights and self-contained source provenance, (b) print and figure production, (c) local errors in important worked examples and adversarial-evaluation methodology, and (d) a gap between the pedagogical single-region TCO model and the canonical scenario's stated multi-region SLA. Some were already documented in the repository's prior release-candidate notes; this review independently checks them and identifies additional issues. **Publication approval must not be inferred from a previous review's “0 new P0/P1” status.**

## 2. High-priority findings: evidence, consequence, and required changes

### P1-01 — License grants and prohibitions can be read inconsistently

**Where:** PDF page 5 (copyright/license); linked repository `LICENSE` at the **exact SHA recorded in this PDF's Edition history**, lines headed “1. You MAY” and “2. You MUST NOT”.

The PDF summarizes a right to redistribute verbatim copies for noncommercial ends. The actual pinned license permits verbatim redistribution of the whole work in §1(3), yet §2(2) separately prohibits publishing, *republishing* or distributing the work “as a book, article, report, or other discrete publication,” in whole or substantial part, without written approval. Those clauses are susceptible to contradictory readings when someone shares an unchanged PDF of the *whole book*: it is both a verbatim copy and a discrete electronic book/publication. This is not resolved by saying the LICENSE is authoritative, because the **operative grant itself** has the ambiguity.

**Why it matters:** Reader rights, Amazon publication rights, and authorized noncommercial distribution should be unambiguous for a commercial print edition. A copyright page must not advertise a permission that the operative text arguably retracts. This is a legal drafting/publisher-review issue, not a model-architecture defect.

**Fix:** Obtain legal/editorial review; explicitly distinguish *private sharing of an unmodified copy* from *publishing or offering a separately marketed edition*. Define whether unaltered whole-book PDF redistribution is permitted. Harmonize the PDF summary and pinned LICENSE; verify Amazon KDP terms and any exclusivity choices. Do not try to resolve legal interpretation by adding another vague disclaimer.

### P1-02 — External source ledger is insufficiently navigable from the physical book

**Where:** PDF pp. 395–405 (printed Appendix pp. 375–385), notably p. 395: `[A-S01]`–`[A-S105]` references moved to `source-verification.md`; pp. 418–421 edition/provenance discussion.

Ideally ship an accessible HTML source index alongside the book.

### P1-03 — Print specification requires a positive line-weight and legibility check

**Where:** Across PDF, especially chart-heavy pp. 43, 110, 138, 167, 264, 336. Official KDP paperback guidelines currently specify **minimum 0.75 pt for lines** and **7 pt minimum font size**, with embedded fonts and 300-DPI raster images.

The vector-object scan found **1,144 drawn elements with positive stroke widths below 0.75 pt** on **381 pages**. These include recurring page rules and many chart gridlines; they are not all critical content, so the count **does not prove KDP will reject the file**. Also, **73 text spans are about 5.98 pt**, but these are short numeric footnote markers rather than body text. The PDF's main body is around 11 pt; no large block of prose under 7 pt was detected.

**Why it matters:** Fine gridlines and rules may be faint or disappear in inexpensive color/black-and-white printing; small superscript markers are not a substitute for visibly readable footnote references.

**Fix:** Enforce ≥0.75 pt on load-bearing graph strokes, axes, connectors, arrows, and table rules; decide whether to thicken the running-head rule. Inspect all figure labels at actual printed size; check numerical footnote marks physically. Test the actual KDP previewer and order the print proof **before** declaring this gate passed. Keep optional decorative gray gridlines distinct from semantic lines if making tradeoffs.

### P2-01 — Chapter 3 MoE active-parameter worked example is not derivable from its own split

**Where:** PDF pp. 56–59 (printed pp. 36–39), Tables 3-2 and 3-3; propagated to pp. 63–65 and Fig. 3.4.

The example describes ~47B total parameters as about **8 × 5.7B = 45.6B routed experts plus ~1.4B always-on**, but uses **~14B active** for two selected experts. From the stated split, active is **2 × 5.7B + 1.4B = 12.8B**, not 14B. The chosen 14B figure may be an intended rounded assumption, but then the expert/shared decomposition must be changed or explicitly decoupled from it.

At the literal 12.8B split, active/total is **27.23%** rather than 29.8%, FP16 active weights **25.6 GB** rather than 28 GB, linear 2N FLOPs **25.6 GFLOP** rather than 28 GFLOP, and ratio versus dense 70B **0.183×** rather than 0.20×. The high-level lesson remains unchanged, but the table's presented derivation is arithmetically inconsistent.

**Fix:** Choose one internally consistent illustrative architecture and recalculate the dependent Table 3-2/Table 3-3 examples and mini-case. A transparent option is “~47B total; 45.6B routed + 1.4B shared; 12.8B active”; make all quantities correspond. This was also noted as a minor open item in the prior release notes; it remains worth fixing because it is a worked arithmetic book.

### P2-02 — Chapter 24 calls a uniform-retrieval null model a “minimum exposure”

**Where:** PDF p. 350 (printed p. 330), §24.3.2.

The model estimates `p=50/48,000`, `k=5`, `Q=10,000`, so expected poisoned retrieval hits are `Qkp ≈52` **under uniform random selection**. It correctly warns that real ranked retrieval is not uniform and that adversaries can increase targeted hits. But it then calls the uniform estimate **“the minimum exposure.”** This is mathematically unjustified: a query distribution disjoint from the poisoned chunks could retrieve zero; an effective targeted attack could retrieve far more. The null expectation is **neither a universal lower nor upper bound**.

**Fix:** Replace “minimum exposure” with “uniform-sampling null reference, not a bound.” Add a two-line counterexample: unrelated queries produce near-zero exposure; poisoned high-similarity content produces elevated exposure. The measurable security metric should be conditioned on query distribution, ranking, chunk provenance, and attack success.

### P2-03 — Chapter 24's lexical deduplication metric is order-dependent

**Where:** PDF p. 349 (printed p. 329), §24.3.1.

PIES uses normalized Levenshtein distance ≤0.25 to assign new prompts to an existing class. But distance threshold proximity is **not transitive**: A may be close to B and B close to C while A is not close to C. A greedy “assign to existing class” algorithm can therefore return different group counts depending on insertion order, choice of representative, and tie-break rules. A threshold alone does **not** fully specify a reproducible partition. The book admits the stated `217` cannot be reproduced from its text; that honesty does not fix the definitional gap.

**Fix:** Either provide the canonical generation order, normalization denominator, representative-selection rule, stable sort, tie-break rule and test fixture, or call PIES an explicitly **order-sensitive heuristic** rather than a deterministic cross-run class count. Avoid conflating lexical pattern diversity with actual injection success.

### P2-04 — P/D disaggregation figure mixes two deployment topologies in one latency example

**Where:** PDF p. 199 (printed p. 179), Fig. 11.3 and caption.

The schematic depicts a **prefill 8×H100 host** and **decode 8×H100 host**, implying inter-host KV transfer. The caption gives a useful `~35 GB / 450 GB/s ≈0.08 s` **on-node NVLink** comparison, and then `~35 GB / 40 GB/s ≈0.9 s` on a fabric. These are valid separate what-if calculations, but the 450-GB/s on-node path does **not apply to two ordinary separate 8-GPU servers** without a separately specified high-bandwidth cross-host NVLink domain. Presenting it next to a two-host topology invites misuse.

**Fix:** Split into (A) within-NVLink-domain prefill/decode separation and (B) cross-host disaggregation, with the appropriate fabric each time. Add aggregate KV migration budget: 35 GB/request at 40 requests/s is **1.4 TB/s of payload across the fleet** before retries/replication; a low per-request transfer time is not sufficient to establish total fabric capacity. Maintain the caveat that the 35-GB number is illustrative raw FP16 KV for the named context/geometry.

### P2-05 — TCO's cloud line across varying volume is not a feasible service-cost curve

**Where:** PDF pp. 254–264 (printed pp. 234–244), Tables 16-1/16-2, Fig. 16.1.

The chapter makes exceptional efforts to disclose noncomparability, which is good. Yet Figure 16.1 includes a flat **$87.6K/month cloud line (4,380 paid host-hours)** over many orders of magnitude of request volume. The chosen fixed cloud occupancy may be a scenario input at the canonical 6.24M requests, but **cannot be presumed to serve every point on the volume axis**, even within the frozen 21-host self-host fleet's feasibility region. The nominal price ranking of the three modes therefore has no supported counterfactual performance equivalence outside the named operating point.

**Fix:** In Fig. 16.1 either plot cloud as a marker at the one priced operating point or omit it from the crossover curve; distinguish the fixed self-host-vs-API algebraic illustration from a realistic load-duration cost comparison. Retain one concise noncomparability warning and reduce repetitive caveat paragraphs elsewhere.

### P2-06 — Canonical SLA remains unsized while the narrative approaches a final choice

**Where:** PDF pp. 256–261 (printed pp. 236–241), §16.6 on p. 263; Chs. 17, 22, 25.

The canonical workload's stated **multi-region active-active 99.9% SLA** is explicitly not included in the single-region $155.8K/21-host / $218.3K/30-host cost model. The chapter correctly says multi-region must be sized and priced separately. But the educational story should not imply that an architecture has passed the **“Validate & Defend / Commit”** gate while a hard nonfunctional requirement remains unsatisfied or unpriced.

**Fix:** Add a prominent decision-state box: `CANDIDATE SCREENED; NOT COMMITTABLE — regional availability, failover, SLO-goodput and matched-scope costs pending`. In Chs. 22 and 25, include an example ADR with an explicit **HOLD** outcome rather than selecting the least-cost option. For regional failover, distinguish mandatory weight/index availability from the **optional policy** to replicate live KV: clients may replay/recompute context instead, subject to recovery objectives. Avoid saying KV replication is categorically required.

### P2-07 — Figure 6.2 visually understates the SLO-violation area

**Where:** PDF p. 110 (printed p. 90), Fig. 6.2.

The graph marks a 2-second latency SLO but shades **≥4 seconds**, not all requests with latency above 2 seconds. The caption explicitly discloses this, so the manuscript is not concealing the distinction; nonetheless the visual encoding is weaker than it could be. A reader scanning without the paragraph may infer the shaded region is the whole SLO breach region.

**Fix:** Shade latency `>2 s` lightly; use a darker hatch for the 5-second straggler cluster. Keep the sample-p99 vs population-p99 lesson, which is technically valuable.

### P2-08 — Overlong captions reverse the book's own three-part caption convention

**Where:** Fig. 16.1 PDF p. 264 (~235 words following the caption marker); Fig. 22.1 p. 336 (~326 words of figure-adjacent text); Figs. 17.1, 7.7, 3.3, 15.2; PDF p. 421's stated caption-style convention.

This improves local caveat coverage but worsens scanability, page economy, and visual priority; the primary takeaway is buried.

**Fix:** One sentence of conclusion; one line of scope/provenance; one critical caveat. Put derivation/limitations in nearby running text, a clearly labeled technical note, or a table note. Preserve essential qualifiers but remove verbal duplication. Use Fig. 16.1 and Fig. 22.1 as the first editorial prototypes.

At US Letter size, some figures occupy only the upper/middle third of the page while full explanatory material continues on another page. ### P3-03 — Keep model-family terminology, counts, and dates pinned

**Where:** Ch. 3, Appendix A pp. 382–409, preface pp. 17–19.

Independent spot checks confirmed first-party descriptions of **Qwen3.8-Flash-Next** as 125B backbone + 51B n-gram embedding + roughly 6B activated, and **DeepSeek V4** vendor-relative 10%/7% or 27%/10% FLOP/KV numbers with model-specific denominators. ### P3-04 — “Reference architecture” vs “production recommendation” language

**Where:** Chs. 7–8, 12–17, 22 and Appendix A.

A deliberately conservative **dense 70B, full-MHA, FP16-KV teaching geometry** is appropriate for transparent arithmetic. It is not a typical 2026 70B production configuration. The book mostly states that boundary correctly. To reduce reader misapplication, label all summary numbers `TEACHING GEOMETRY — RE-DERIVE FOR REAL MODEL` rather than relying on a distant caveat. This is especially important when readers copy the 21-host peak and ~2.62MB/token numbers into slides or purchase justifications.

## 8. Proposed copy-ready corrections

### 8.1 Chapter 24, uniform-retrieval exposure

> **Uniform-sampling reference, not a bound.** For illustration only, if each of 10,000 queries retrieves five chunks uniformly from a 48,000-chunk corpus containing 50 poisoned chunks, the expected number of poisoned retrievals is `10,000 × 5 × 50/48,000 ≈ 52`. Actual ranked retrieval is query- and embedding-dependent: exposure may be near zero or far above this null reference. Measure it on realistic query distributions and adversarial fixtures; do not treat the uniform result as a minimum, safety bound or attack-success rate.

### 8.2 Chapter 3, MoE parameter mass

> **Illustrative arithmetic.** Eight routed experts of 5.7B parameters each plus 1.4B shared parameters give 47.0B resident parameters. If the router selects two experts per token, the approximate active parameter mass is `2 × 5.7B + 1.4B = 12.8B`, assuming every shared parameter participates. That corresponds to about 27% of the resident parameters. Expert-sharing, attention, embedding and router details must be enumerated separately for any real model.

### 8.3 Chapter 16 / Chapter 22, SLO-qualified decision

> **Decision status: PROVISIONAL — not deployment approved.** The single-region 21-host/30-host numbers and frozen-fleet cost crossover are transparent planning calculations, not a demonstrated 99.9% active-active multi-region architecture. Procurement and production commitment remain blocked pending regional redundancy/failure-domain design, matched-scope TCO, representative offered-load traces, and measured tail-latency/quality-qualified goodput.

### 8.4 Figure 11.3, topology note

> **Transfer location matters.** The on-node NVLink example estimates a transfer within an appropriate connected accelerator domain. The two-server diagram instead requires cross-host transport; its available payload throughput, protocol overhead, and competing transfers must be measured on the actual interconnect. At 35 GB transferred per request and a peak 40 requests/s, offered transfer volume is 1.4 TB/s aggregate, before any replication or retries.
