## A. Release-blocking and high-priority findings

### P1-01 — DeepSeek V4-Pro service status is out of date (CONFIRMED)

**PDF pp. 391, 395 (Appendix A; service-status caveat and §A.3).** The PDF says that, starting September 14, 2026, `deepseek-v4-pro` requests route to V4.1-Flash at V4.1-Flash rates. This reproduces the original September 10 marketing announcement, but DeepSeek's *current official API change log* says it **decided to continue DeepSeek V4-Pro API service after September 14, with unchanged billing**. DeepSeek V4-Flash and V4-Flash-Vision-Exp retirement is separately confirmed. The conflicting official records can be viewed at:
- Official announcement: https://www.deepseek.com/en/news/deepseek-v4-1-flash/
- Official current API change log: https://api-docs.deepseek.com/updates/

**Required:** Edit both passages, explicitly noting announcement → superseding service-status correction. Don't conflate V4-Flash API alias routing with V4-Pro availability. Give the service-status verification date. The August 30 frozen architectural comparison may stay as historical evidence; the September status note must be corrected.

### P1-02 — Canonical prefill FLOPs are computed on different bases across chapters (CONFIRMED arithmetic; conditional execution model)

**PDF pp. 34, 42, 151–153, 241, 260–262, 322, 341–343.** Chapter 8 correctly calls its **1.288 PFLOP/request** value a *parameterized-linear-only* estimate, `2NL`, explicitly excluding quadratic attention. Figure 22.1 and PDF page 342, however, separately assert the quadratic term `4 × layers × L² × hidden_width` for standard full-matrix attention, with a 17% addition at 9,200 tokens. Chapter 22 and some fleet/TCO summaries still present **~1.29 PFLOP/request** without qualifying that it excludes the chapter's own attention term.

Under **the PDF's own full-matrix formula**:

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---:|
| Linear parameter term | `2 × 70×10^9 × 9,200` | **1.288 PFLOP/request** |
| Explicit attention term | `4 × 80 × 9,200² × 8,192` | **0.2219 PFLOP/request** |
| Model's combined prefill estimate | sum | **1.5099 PFLOP/request** |
| Difference | attention / linear | **+17.23%** |

The distinction is load-bearing: Chapters 8, 16, 17, and 20 use the smaller value to produce an independent **19-host prefill sizing screen** at 40 rps using **35% MFU**. If the modeled attention term is included at the same assumed compute efficiency, the requirement becomes **~21.81 hosts → at least 22 hosts**, prefill-only. This is one more than the **21-host KV screen** and changes which screen binds.

At a **70% provisioning-utilization target plus N−1 reserve**, applying the same aggregate model gives `ceil(21.81 / 0.70) + 1 = 33 hosts`, not the 30-host KV-only policy. These are **illustrative planning calculations**, *not measured fleet requirements*. Actual causal kernels may skip masked triangular work, reducing the coefficient; measured MFU and prefill/decode coexistence can change the answer further. **That is exactly why the book must state which compute model is used rather than mixing them.**

**Required:** Adopt a single well-defined prefill accounting convention (e.g., linear-only *lower bound*, full-square attention *upper/conservative model*, and causal-triangle kernel sensitivity), recompute the independent compute screen and all cited host numbers, and explicitly label the dependence on kernel, MFU, and decode interference. Do **not** present either 21 or 22 as achieved SLO-goodput absent measurement. Use the book's own modeling choices consistently across Fig. 22.1, Ch. 8, Ch. 16, Ch. 17, and Ch. 20.

### P1-03 — TCO decision figures depend on the possibly under-sized 21-host base (CONFIRMED dependency)

**PDF pp. 75–76, 254–267, Fig. 16.1, and pp. 271–278.** The self-host **$155,816.93/month**, **$24.97/1K requests**, and **~7.5M requests/month** self-host/API crossover are internally consistent *given* the fixed 21-host assumption and $20.80/1K API example. But after P1-02, the **baseline choice of 21 hosts is not independently reconciled to the full prefill compute model**. The economics are valid as an intentionally frozen *21-host hypothetical*, **not yet as the least feasible canonical host-count result**. Chapter 16 already distinguishes incomplete regional availability and unmeasured goodput; extend that same discipline to the compute screen.

**Required:** Keep the original 21-host run as an explicitly labeled sensitivity if pedagogically useful. Add recomputed 22-host and correctly headroom/failover-sized scenarios using the actual TCO model. Regenerate Fig. 16.1 and all linked references if the baseline changes. Preserve staff as a separate fixed cost rather than scaling the total 21-host cost proportionally.

### P2-04 — Single-GPU versus eight-GPU comparison loses its scope in Chapter 22 (CONFIRMED editorial precision)

**PDF p. 342.** The text says `~1.29 PFLOP/request → ~1.19 PFLOPS required at the ~1.08 s budget vs H100 ~0.989 PFLOPS peak`. This is a legitimate demonstration that **one** H100 alone cannot meet the linear-only deadline, a distinction made carefully earlier (p. 42). But the surrounding Chapter 22 discussion is about the **canonical 8×H100 host**. A reader could mistake 1.19 PFLOPS as per-GPU demand on that host or conclude the host itself falls short. Nor is 1.29 the entire prefill cost under Fig. 22.1.

**Required:** Label `1.19 PFLOPS` as *aggregate, linear-only deadline demand* and compare only against an equally scoped aggregate host peak, or explicitly isolate a separate **single-GPU counterfactual**. Keep regime classification (arithmetic intensity/roofline) separate from deadline and capacity feasibility.

### P2-05 — Full-matrix versus causally triangular attention is not normalized in the crossover claim (CONDITIONAL)

**PDF pp. 241, 341–342, Fig. 22.1.** The **53.4K-token crossover** follows algebraically from `4 n_layers L² d` versus `2NL`. That `4L²` coefficient corresponds to evaluating full-square QK/AV pair work; a causal implementation that skips unavailable upper-triangular positions has approximately half the leading attention-pair term, shifting the same idealized crossover toward **~106.8K tokens**, all else fixed. Thus 53.4K is a **conditional work-model crossover**, not a universal property of dense autoregressive attention. Some kernels compute a different amount of masked work, and the model's attention geometry matters.

**Required:** State the FLOP counting convention in Fig. 22.1 and its derivation. Either include a causal-triangle sensitivity curve or clearly write “full-square attention work approximation.” This is particularly important because the book promises architecture-defensible arithmetic rather than Big-O alone.

### P2-06 — Appendix A equates qualitative interpretation with arithmetic DERIVED status (CONFIRMED internal policy mismatch)

**PDF p. 394 (§A.2), compared with pp. 17, 424–426.** Appendix A maps `Interpretation ↔ [DERIVED]`, even though the book defines DERIVED as a number computed by arithmetic from explicit inputs. An inference such as “efficiency improvements are mostly in context and cost” may be a plausible *interpretation*, but is not an arithmetic result. Labeling it DERIVED implies a stronger evidentiary status than the declared taxonomy warrants.

**Required:** Use an explicit **ANALYSIS / INTERPRETATION** prose marker that is *not* a sixth arithmetic status; use `[HYPOTHESIS]` when the interpretation makes a testable causal or deployment prediction. Reserve `[DERIVED]` for reproducible calculations. Add an example contrasting numeric ratio, descriptive interpretation, and measurable prediction.

## B. Additional substantive editorial and production findings

### P2-07 — The canonical example does not close the availability requirement
**PDF pp. 75–76 and 260–262; Chs. 16–17.** The canonical service requires multi-region active-active 99.9%, while its numerical TCO and 21-/30-host planning rows are single-region. The manuscript commendably marks multi-region as **UNSIZED / NOT PRICED / NOT VALIDATED**. This is an **intentional stated limitation, not a mathematical contradiction**, but it leaves the flagship “defensible architecture” without an actually closable deployment/price decision. Provide a small *parametric* multi-region worksheet (regional traffic share, surviving-region 100% traffic, warmed capacity, replication/RPO/RTO, failover tests, cost) even if no hypothetical final price is asserted. Keep “candidate only” language.

### P2-08 — Chapter 16 mini-case switches time basis without explaining it
**PDF pp. 266–268.** The startup is said to average ~2 rps and process ~5M requests/month. This implies roughly 29 days of 24×7 requests (`5M / 2 ≈ 2.5M s`). The canonical case elsewhere prices its 10 rps over **8 hours × 260 workdays** (624,000 seconds/month). The mini-case is valid only if its different duty window is explicit. Add “a different startup workload with 24×7 traffic” and show a one-line rate reconciliation. Otherwise a reader may incorrectly carry the canonical business-hours denominator into the startup case.

### P2-09 — Staff cost and opportunity cost need a non-overlapping ledger
**PDF p. 268.** The startup's ~$40K/month self-host cost already contains about ~$26K/month for 0.5 FTE. The narrative then requires product opportunity cost above ~$64K/month to reverse a $64K direct cost advantage. Clarify whether the additional $64K is **incremental foregone product value beyond the included compensation**, or an alternative valuation of those same hours. Without this, a reader may double-count labor. A small table separating wages, time redeployment, and incremental foregone contribution would fix it.

### P2-10 — Figure A.1's “total parameter” comparisons use differing denominators
**PDF pp. 392–393.** The figure labels Qwen's **6B/125B** as a backbone-only fraction (excluding the separate 51B n-gram embedding and 4B MTP modules), while some other series are phrased as total-parameter fractions. The paragraph discloses the exception, which is good; however, the figure's headline “total vs activated parameters” suggests stronger apples-to-apples denominators than exist. Label the denominator column explicitly (“vendor-reported backbone / resident total; varies by vendor”) and mark the Qwen bar as **backbone ratio**, not total system parameter ratio.

### P2-11 — Front-matter and figure captions overburden first-pass readability
**PDF pp. 12–19 and selected caption-heavy pages 28, 154, 168, 267, 398.** The provenance discipline is rigorous, but the reader encounters multiple pages of conventions, caveats, addenda and labeling before reaching the first chapter. Several figure captions repeat modeling caveats already in adjacent technical notes; Fig. 16.1's caption is a near-paragraph-length proof key. The style convention on PDF p. 430 says captions should be takeaway + scope/source + measurement warning. Enforce it. Move lengthy qualifications to numbered figure notes in the main text; keep 1–3-sentence captions with scope clearly visible, and retain all evidence information in footnotes or source ledgers.

### P2-12 — Figure 1.2 encourages a one-way KV-pipeline reading
**PDF p. 28.** It visually treats “KV cache” as the endpoint after attention, although the KV cache is also **read during** every applicable attention step. The caption accurately warns of the abstraction and points to Fig. 2.1. This mitigation is welcome but not ideal for self-contained visual pedagogy. Add an explicit backward/read arrow from cache to attention, distinguish persistent state from processing stage, or reduce the caption claim to “one-time token-to-state construction.”

### P2-13 — Print diagrams need a measurable accessibility gate
**PDF pp. 139, 154, 168, 267, 329, 382, 392, 398, 401.** Several plots contain dense miniature text, annotation boxes, long scope disclaimers, and colored traces or legends. The full-size PDF is generally sharp and many diagrams are vector graphics, but the actual printed figure font size and grayscale differentiation need a physical proof. Set minimum plotted label size (prefer ≥8 pt at **final physical trim scale**, with ≥7 pt only for minor ticks); distinguish data series by line style/markers/patterns, not color alone; test on monochrome printing at 100% and at the intended KDP trim size. Do not solve tiny legends with a longer caption.

### P2-14 — Print edition needs explicit cover/interior separation
**PDF p. 1, front matter pp. 2–6.** The book PDF begins with a cover-like title graphic, followed by intentional blanks and a separate text title page. This can be reasonable for a digital reading PDF, but Amazon paperback production ordinarily needs a **separate full-wrap cover asset** and a specifically proofed **interior PDF**. Verify that the actual KDP interior does not inadvertently duplicate the separate physical cover and that front matter, page parity, blank pages and spine/gutter agree with the intended print specification. Do not assume the current all-in-one PDF is the ready-to-upload interior.

### P3-15 — List of Figures exposes auto-generated wording
**PDF p. 10.** Figure 22.1's entry is truncated as “Takeaway: the quadratic attention term overtakes the…”. A deliberately short optional list-of-figures title would read better (e.g., “Attention-vs-projection prefill FLOP crossover”). Ensure titles are statements, not auto-cropped first lines of long descriptive captions.

### P3-16 — Figure A.3 and A.4 are conceptual, not measured progression or a ranked stack
**PDF pp. 401–402.** The diagrams themselves contain disclaimers but can be misread as a historical maturity ladder or as the placement of intelligence being a universally ordered hierarchy. Add a visual axis label such as “selected illustrative mechanism milestones (not comprehensive chronology)” and “nonexclusive placement choices—not a ranking.” This reduces the explanatory burden on small captions.

### P3-17 — The conclusion's “measurement reduces to token counting” is too narrow
**PDF pp. 342–343 (22.4).** In the context of Chapter 22's cross-layer method, “For this chapter, measurement reduces to counting tokens correctly” underplays the benchmark, SLO-goodput, availability, and TCO validation gates that the book insists on elsewhere. Replace with “For the token-accounting step of the method…” and cross-link to the actual end-to-end measurement gates. Retain the useful tokenization checklist.

### P3-18 — The appendix is dated but model-service notes should be operationally separable
**PDF pp. 390–415.** The manuscript commendably isolates August vendor architecture claims from September and October addenda. Readers are nevertheless forced to read several pages to distinguish static family specifications from dynamic aliases/endpoint availability. Provide a compact `Architecture snapshot / Current API serving status / Independent reproduction` table, with separate dates; reverify service aliases on release day without reclassifying the frozen August architecture evidence.
