figure 7.4: The concurrency budget: where a 70B (canonical 80-layer full-MHA teaching geometry) 
host’s 640 GB pool goes. Aggregate-feasibility caveat: the 640 GB figure is an aggregate across 
eight 80-GB H100s, not a single freely-allotable heap. Whether a given allocation actually fits depends 
on tensor-parallel sharding, KV partitioning, replication, runtime layout, per-rank fragmentation, 
workspace requirements, and communication topology — so “total bytes < total HBM” is a necessary 
but not sufficient test. Per-rank fit and sharding must also be validated (Chapters 9–10). Per-rank 
example (TP=8): the 140 GB of weights shard to ~17.5 GB per GPU; the ~64 GB runtime/NCCL 
reserve is per-host (not per-rank); the ~436 GB KV budget shards to ~54.5 GB per GPU, and under 
tensor-parallel attention the per-request KV (24.9 GB) also shards across the 8 ranks, so the per-rank 
KV per request is ~3.1 GB — the 17.5 concurrent figure is the aggregate bound, and the per-rank 
fit must be checked separately (a rank can be the binding constraint even when the aggregate fits). 
The figure shows the one dominant budget relationship: the on-host pool decomposes into weights 
+ runtime + a leftover KV budget, and that KV budget divided by the per-request KV price sets 
concurrency. 140 GB weights + ~64 GB runtime/NCCL leaves ~436 GB of KV budget; at the 24.9 
GB/request max-FP16 KV that gives C ≈ 17.5 concurrent requests (byte-accurate 436÷24.9037 = 17.51; 
the conservative integer “requests that fit” floor is 17, not 18), and FP8 (~12.45 GB/request) roughly 
raises it to ~35.0. This is the arithmetic behind the single-host capacity in Ch17. This is an analytical 
upper bound, not a validated concurrent-request count — the actual achievable concurrency 
must be measured under the target workload (Ch 14).
