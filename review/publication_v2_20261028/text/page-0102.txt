• Prefill is compute-bound at the canonical long-prompt operating point. The reason 
prefill lands on the compute plateau of the roofline is its high arithmetic intensity: the FLOPs per 
byte moved are far above the ~295 FLOP/byte ridge point (developed in Ch 8), so the compute 
units — not the HBM weight stream — are the binding resource. Only after establishing that 
regime do we check the deadline: the prefill pass for 9,200 input tokens costs ≈ 2 ×70e9 params 
× 9.2e3 tokens ≈ 1.29 PFLOP, which against the ~1.08 s prefill budget implies a required rate of 
≈ 1.19 PFLOPS — above a single H100’s ~0.989 PFLOPS dense BF16 peak (1.19 > 0.989). But that 
1.19-vs-0.989 comparison is a single-device deadline/capacity illustration, not the evidence 
that prefill is compute-bound: on the canonical 8×H100 host the aggregate peak is ~7.9 PFLOPS, 
so the host comfortably satisfies the rate — the “single H100 can’t prefill a 9.2K prompt in 1.08 s” 
conclusion is about whether one arbitrarily-chosen GPU can meet the deadline, not about the 
kernel’s roofline regime. Prefill being compute-bound (rather than bandwidth-bound) follows 
from its high arithmetic intensity, not from the 1.19-vs-0.989 comparison. [canonical scenario]

At the resource layer, the tell is utilization: a decode kernel pinned at ~95% of HBM bandwidth 
but ~30% of FLOPs is bandwidth-bound; a prefill kernel with ~90% FLOP utilization is compute-bound. 
The same hardware, the same model — the boundary flips between prefill and decode. This 
is precisely why Splitwise/DistServe (and Mooncake’s KV-centric disaggregation) split prefill from 
decode onto different pools: the bottleneck-regime difference justifies it. [S4][1P][FACT] (source
