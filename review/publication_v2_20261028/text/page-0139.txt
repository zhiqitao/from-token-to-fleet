9.2. Mental Model
The interconnect is best read as a series of concentric rings. The GPU sits at the center, reached 
by the fewest hops (PCIe). One ring out is the node: GPUs on the same server are joined by NVLink 
and NVSwitch, forming a high-radix, low-latency mesh. The next ring out is the rack: 100/200/400 
GbE RoCE connects nodes. The outermost ring is the cluster or data center: InfiniBand HDR (200 
Gb/s) or NDR (400 Gb/s) stitches racks into a single logical network. A well-designed deployment 
keeps the heavy collectives (all-reduce, reduce-scatter) on the inner rings and reserves the outer 
rings for less volume or latency-tolerant traffic.
The arithmetic is relentless. If all-reduce moves 140 GB and the NVLink bisection provides 900 
GB/s of total bidirectional bandwidth per GPU (450 GB/s per direction; 18 NVLink 4.0 links × 50 
GB/s bidirectional, NVIDIA H100 datasheet), a lower bound on time is 140 GB ÷ 900 GB/s ≈ 0.16 s 
[1P][DERIVED] — note the word lower bound. The actual completion time of a real collective is 
higher because a collective is not one flat transfer but a sequence of message-passing and reduction 
steps across the topology:
Tcollective ≈α × nsteps + bytes
β
where α is the per-step latency (message setup, synchronization), β is the achieved (not peak) 
bandwidth, and nsteps is the number of message-passing/reduction hops. (For a flat transfer nsteps = 1.) 
The 0.16 s figure uses only the bytes/β term at peak bandwidth; real NCCL all-reduce on NVLink 
lands tens of % above it once per-step overhead, ring stages, and topology routing are included. 
So treat the bytes ÷ bandwidth form as a sizing lower bound, and reserve measured nccl-tests 
numbers (§9.4 Measurement) for capacity decisions. The relative ranking between interconnects 
is what matters most here: if those same 140 GB travel over a 200 Gb/s HDR InfiniBand link (≈ 
25 GB/s effective unidirectional) it is ≈ 5.6 s — ~35× longer; over 25 Gb/s Ethernet (≈ 2.5 GB/s 
practical-effective, §9.3) ≈ 56 s. (These are flat single-rail lower bounds, 140 GB ÷ bandwidth. The 
§9.3 ring-model worked example uses the multi-rail effective bandwidth (40 GB/s) plus the 1.75 ring 
factor, giving ≈ 6.1 s (IB) and ≈ 98 s (RoCE) — the same ranking, a slightly higher absolute time.) The 
interconnect choice is a first-order determinant of wall-clock time in all cases.
