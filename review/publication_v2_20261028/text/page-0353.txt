Why [ILLUSTRATIVE] appears on two axes. It is the same worked-example number viewed from 
two axes that answer different questions: on Axis 1 it is the provenance (an input we chose so the 
arithmetic would be concrete), on Axis 3 it is the stage (a worked-example value, not a measurement 
or a vendor claim). The two uses never conflict — a worked-example input is by definition at the 
ILLUSTRATIVE stage — so [ILLUSTRATIVE] is one token shared by two orthogonal axes, not an 
ambiguity.
Scope of the labeling. The label is mandatory for every load-bearing quantitative claim — a number 
that a later calculation, sizing decision, or comparison depends on; the Axis-3 stage rides with every 
load-bearing claim whose stage is known. Such a claim carries the labels directly (inline) or via an 
identified table or section that states them for every row. Purely illustrative or rhetorical numbers 
that no later calculation depends on (e.g. a round figure used only to make a sentence concrete) may 
carry the labels in the surrounding prose rather than inline. This is the unambiguous exception the 
preface refers to: the test is whether a downstream number depends on it.
Reading a label. [1P][FACT] means a vendor-stated fact; [1P][DERIVED] means arithmetic we 
computed from a first-party input; [LAB][FACT] means a home-lab measurement; [ILLUSTRA-
TIVE][ASSUMPTION] means a number we chose so the worked example would be concrete — it is 
not evidence of anything about the real workload. The Axis-3 measurement status reads alongside 
them: a bare [ANALYTICAL] means an arithmetic upper bound from stated inputs (valid for planning, 
not an achieved rate); [MODELED] means the result of a simulation or queueing model (it reflects 
the model, not a measured system); [MEASURED] means a load test with a recorded trace that 
demonstrates an achieved rate and tail-latency SLO on real hardware; [UNMEASURED] means the 
stage is not yet determined, so the value cannot be treated as achieved throughput; and the combined 
[LAB][MEASURED] pairs the Axis-1 provenance (our own hardware) with that Axis-3 stage. When 
only one axis is shown, the other is implied by the surrounding sentence (e.g. a bare [DERIVED] 
carries the status, with the provenance stated in prose).
The [S#] keys are a bibliography index, not a provenance. A label such as [S6][1P] combines 
a source reference key ([S6], pointing to an entry in the References chapter) with a provenance 
on that source ([1P]). The [S#] key is not a fifth provenance item; it merely says which reference 
the first-party or secondary evidence comes from. A composite label always carries at least one 
provenance item in addition to any [S#] key.
Prices, models, and benchmarks are a moving target. The model names, parameter counts, 
context limits, prices, and throughput figures in this book are a dated snapshot. They vary with 
the provider, region, commit/on-demand/spot status, hardware SKU, precision and sparsity mode, 
quantization, batch and concurrency, software stack, GPU count and topology, kernel version, warmup 
procedure, and percentile method used. Re-verify before acting.
