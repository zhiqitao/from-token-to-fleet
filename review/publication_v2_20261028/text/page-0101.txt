6.3.2. Goodput vs Raw Throughput
Deploying a higher-throughput configuration can reduce goodput. Concretely: suppose we enable 
larger dynamic batching to raise raw tokens/s from 50 tok/s to 70 tok/s, but the batch window pushes 
p95 TTFT from 1.8 s to 2.3 s — above the 2 s SLO. The extra 20 tok/s are produced, but they land in the 
tail that misses the SLO, so goodput does not grow. Raw throughput is up 40%; goodput (tokens that 
met the SLO) is effectively unchanged in this case, because the batch of SLO-compliant tokens didn’t 
grow. (The general relation is goodput = raw_rate × compliant_fraction: if the compliant fraction 
falls from 96% to 94%, goodput is 50×0.96 = 48 vs 70×0.94 = 65.8 — it rises despite the p95 breach. 
The “unchanged” claim holds only when the extra tokens all land in the SLO-missing tail, i.e. the 
compliant fraction drops proportionally to the raw-rate gain. A p95 breach is a configuration-level 
acceptance signal; request-level SLO-goodput is a separate measurement and must be computed 
from the compliant fraction, not inferred from the p95 alone.) Goodput is the metric the customer 
experiences; raw throughput is what the GPU achieves. The architect optimizes goodput. This is 
why “faster” hardware or bigger batches do not trivially mean a better system.
6.3.3. Detecting the Regime: Bandwidth-Bound vs Compute-Bound
The most decision-relevant resource question is: is this layer bandwidth-bound or compute-bound?

• Decode is HBM-bandwidth-bound at low batch. Generating one token needs a full forward 
pass over the 70B weights of the canonical 80-layer, hidden-8192, full-MHA 70B reference 
= 140 GB (FP16) of reads per token — a batch-1-equivalent idealized weight-streaming read 
(one request’s full weight pass), not a measured bytes-physically-read figure. Within a ~25 
ms TPOT budget that demands 140 GB / 0.025 s ≈ 5.6 TB/s (batch-1-equivalent) of HBM

8×H100 host’s actual deployment verdict. Two scope notes keep this honest. First, 5.6 TB/s 
is the aggregate model-wide weight-read rate for a hypothetical single-device implementation; 
under tensor parallelism (the canonical 8×H100 host) the 140 GB is sharded, so each GPU
