Evidence cutoff and edition date. This edition is build v20260913 (2026-09-13). The single 
evidence cutoff for it is 2026-08-30: model, vendor, and hardware claims are stated as of that date, 
and the “As of 2026-08” markers in the body refer to this same cutoff. Primary sources (arXiv reports, 
model cards, vendor pages) were accessed as of the build date; each is cited with a stable identifier in

rather than inheriting the prior value.

First-hand Sources list, and is labeled a first-person capability probe rather than a primary-source 
claim. Every other assertion in the book is a snapshot claim as of 2026-08-30.
Build identifier and generation. The build identifier v20260913 is the content edition: it names the 
source snapshot and evidence cutoff that the text reflects. Re-rendering that same source to a PDF 
on a later date (e.g. a proof generated in October) does not change the content edition and does not 
extend the evidence cutoff; it only changes the PDF’s generation timestamp, which is recorded in 
the document metadata and is not evidence. A change to the content requires a new build identifier, 
which invalidates the prior edition and is recorded in the release log — except a dated post-cutoff 
addendum carried under its own revision identifier and listed in the Appendix addenda row.
Release log. The revision that produced this PDF is recorded at the end of the Sources, Method, 
and Reproducibility chapter, in the “Edition history” panel, along with the source revision (commit), 
build identifier, evidence cutoff, appendix addenda, and generation date.
Reading conventions
Three conventions keep the book consistent and honest about its own limits.
Terminology. For the capacity arithmetic in this handbook, GPU memory and VRAM are 
used generically for accelerator-local memory; HBM refers specifically to high-bandwidth-memory 
implementations, which is what the worked examples assume. TTFT, time-to-first-token and prefill 
latency likewise; RAG and retrieval-augmented-generation are the same. Where a distinction is 
architecturally load-bearing — prefill versus decode, total versus active parameters, model capacity 
versus system capacity — the book holds it sharply. Synonyms are a readability concession; load-
bearing contrasts are never relaxed (Ch. 2, 8, 17).
