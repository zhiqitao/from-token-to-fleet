figure 11.3: P/D disaggregation topology: a prefill-optimized pool (FLOPs) and a decode-optimized 
pool (HBM) bridged by KV transfer. The KV-transfer arrow is the load-bearing cost: it moves the per-
request KV state (e.g. ~35 GB for a ~13K-token context at 2.62 MB/token, §9.8) over the interconnect, 
and the disaggregation is only worth it when the prefill/decode specialization benefit exceeds the 
KV-transfer time plus the orchestration overhead of routing requests between two pools. Illustrative: 
at NVLink’s 450 GB/s per direction (§9.2) that ~35 GB transfer is ~0.08 s, which is small against the 
~1.08 s prefill it enables, so the inequality holds on-node; over a 40 GB/s fabric it is ~0.9 s, which can 
erase the benefit — the transfer cost is the term that decides whether P/D disaggregation pays.
The KV moves once per request, then stays local (ownership splits); the split itself exists because the two 
stages want opposite resources under load — prefill needs compute capacity (~1.19 PFLOPS required vs 
0.989 peak single-H100), while decode needs HBM bandwidth (the whole-model weight-read rate of ~5.6 
TB/s if served by one device vs 3.35 TB/s per GPU, sharded to ~0.70 TB/s per GPU under the canonical 
8×H100 host, Ch 6) [1P][DERIVED].
