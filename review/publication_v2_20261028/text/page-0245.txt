Operating Multiple Models
• Tier 2 (70B 8-bit dense): Handles 15% of queries (order status, policy look-up). Runs on 
1×H100. Cost: $0.0015/token, latency ~250 ms.
• Tier 3 (70B FP16): Handles 5% of queries (complex escalations, multilingual). Runs on 2 GPUs 
reserved. Cost: $0.003/token, latency ~120 ms.
Arithmetic: A small pilot deployment serving ~1,000 registered users at a modest, deliberately 
low volume — 5,000 tokens/hour (each request averaging ~250 tokens gives roughly 20 requests/hour, 
a very light load chosen to make the per-token routing economics concrete rather than to model a 
busy fleet; the traffic rate is not what drives this arithmetic, the per-token volume is). Hourly tokens: 
~4,500 in + 500 out = 5,000. (Scope note: these tier per-token rates are illustrative scenario values that 
differ from the §18.3.2 per-1M ledger — they are not the ledger’s $1.25 (8-bit) / $2.50 (FP16) / $0.15 
(4-bit) per-1M rates converted, and the 4-bit tier’s ~600 ms latency differs from the ledger’s 800 ms. They 
are on a per-token basis, distinct from — and not directly comparable to — the per-1M-token ledger; the 
mini-case uses them to demonstrate the routing economics, and a real deployment would price from the 
§18.3.2 per-1M basis.)
• Tier 1 processes 4,000 tokens/hr → $0.80
• Tier 2 processes 750 tokens/hr → $1.13
• Tier 3 processes 250 tokens/hr → $0.75
• Total hourly cost: $2.68 → $1,956 / month (× 730 hr).
Check the SLO before the cost claim. If we had used a single 70B FP16 model: 5,000 tokens/hr 
× $0.003 = $15/hr → $10,950 / month (× 730 hr). The three-tier split looks like an $8,994 / month 
(≈82%) saving — but the SLO gates the cost claim, and here it fails. Tier 1 (the cheapest, 4-bit 
GGUF on CPU, ~600 ms) serves 80% of queries, so the p95 per-request latency is ~600 ms, 
which violates the 500 ms SLO for the dominant tier. The split saves cost by routing the bulk to 
a slow-but-cheap model — precisely the mean-vs-tail trap: the average latency looks fine, yet the 
majority tier misses the deadline. The $8,994 / month saving is therefore conditional on first making 
Tier 1 feasible.
Recalculated cost after substituting a feasible Tier 1. Replace the 4-bit GGUF (CPU, ~600 
ms) with 8-bit quantization on a GPU (~250 ms, $0.0015/token — the same rate as Tier 2, which is 
also 8-bit on 1×H100). The 80% of queries now route at $0.0015/token:
• Tier 1 (8-bit on GPU) processes 4,000 tokens/hr → $6.00
• Tier 2 (70B 8-bit dense) processes 750 tokens/hr → $1.13
• Tier 3 (70B FP16) processes 250 tokens/hr → $0.75
• Total hourly cost: $7.88 → $5,752 / month (× 730 hr).
