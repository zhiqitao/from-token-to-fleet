KV cache memory footprint: The KV cache grows linearly with cached context and layers; it 
must never be computed per-layer and then treated as the whole-model total. For the canonical 
full-MHA reference geometry (80 layers, hidden dimension 8,192 — the 70B-class teaching model, not 
a universal 70B property), the per-token footprint across ALL layers is 2 × layers × hidden × bytes = 
2 ×80 ×8,192 ×2 = 2,621,440 B ≈ 2.62 MB/token (2.5 MiB; canonical value from Chapter 7) — not the 
32 KB that a single-layer calculation would suggest. For a 9,500-token context, one user’s KV cache is 
therefore ≈9,500 ×2.62 MB ≈ 24.9 GB, not ~304 MB. For the canonical workload’s ~86 average in-flight 
requests at 10 rps (L_exec = λ × W_exec ≈ 10 × 8.6; see Ch 4 — note ~86 is the request occupancy, 
distinct from the ~100 active-user population), the total KV cache would be ≈86 × 24.9 GB ≈ 2,140 GB 
— which does not fit the 640 GB GPU pool; using the canonical practical KV budget (640 GB − 140 GB 
weights − ~64 GB runtime/workspace ≈ 436 GB), only ≈17.5 concurrent requests (9,500-token KV each) 
fit on-host for KV (436 GB ÷ 24.9037 GB; conservative integer floor 17 (aggregate bound; per-rank 
and fragmentation must be checked separately)), before also reserving weight residency. This is 
a decisive, order-of-magnitude correction to the intuitive picture: long-context KV is a first-order 
memory constraint, and the architect sizes concurrency by KV capacity, not by the small per-layer 
number. Doubling context to 19,000 tokens pushes per-user KV to ~49.8 GB (19,000 × 2.62 MB) and 
halves on-host concurrency to ~8.7 (436 GB ÷ 49.8 GB; conservative integer floor 8). The canonical 
unit-check rule (quantity/unit/formula/substitution/result/sanity) catches this class of error: if 9,500 
tokens × ~2.5 MB/token ≈ 24 GB does not reconcile with the intended ~304 MB, the unit layer-count 
has been dropped.
Attention FLOP comparison: Scaled‑dot‑product attention (SDPA) for 9,200‑token prompts. Note 
the 2P/token figure above approximates the parameterized dense‑matmul work per token (2 × pa­
rameters); it does not capture all sequence‑length‑dependent attention work, which is accounted 
for separately here. Attention is two matmuls per token per head — Q·KT then ·V — each a mul­
tiply‑accumulate of L²·d, so in FLOPs (2 per MAC) the per‑layer cost is ≈ 4 × L² × d_model, where 
L=9,200 and d_model=8,192. That is 4 × 9,200² × 8,192 ≈ 2.8 × 10¹² FLOPs per layer (the full 64 heads; 
the per‑head figure 4 × 9,200² × 128 ≈ 4.3 × 10¹⁰ must be multiplied by the number of heads, not 
quoted alone). Across 80 layers that is ≈ 2.2 × 10¹⁴ FLOPs — the quadratic attention term that grows 
dominant at long context, matching Chapter 8’s 4·n_layers·L²·d formula: the attention term is ≈17% 
of the parameter-matmul FLOPs (≈15% of total forward FLOPs) at 9.2K (Fig 22.1).
