18.3.5. Consolidation vs. separation decision
• Consolidate when: workload profile overlap ≥70%, latency tolerance ≥300 ms, and cost differ­
ential >30%. Verified: our scenario sits exactly at the threshold — it consolidates ~70% of traffic 
to 8-bit (at the ≥70% boundary, not above it), saving ~$11.16/hr ($23.75 − $12.59, the §18.3.3 
derived figure).
• Keep separate when: (a) security/classification policies require isolation, (b) workloads have 
bimodal latency needs (some ≤50 ms, others batch-tolerant), or (c) model versioning cadence 
differs (e.g., frequent fine-tuning on one family, stable other).
In our example, consolidation wins because the MoE and 8-bit models capture the same request 
classes at lower cost, and the FP16 tier handles the tail without contention. The FP16 tier has no 
separate reserve in this worked example: per §18.3.1 it shares the 640 GB pool — 140 GB of weights 
plus KV — and at the canonical 9.5K profile it cannot be a 2-GPU reserve, because a 2-GPU shard 
holds 160 GB − 140 GB = 20 GB of KV headroom against the 24.9 GB that one 9,500-token request 
needs (κ = 2.62 MB/token, Ch 17). Two reserved GPUs suffice only for the ~250-token support queries 
of §18.8, not for the canonical 9.5K request.
