The architect does not discard Candidate (c) by parameter counting alone: it is provisionally 
moved to the quality-gate evaluation (Chapter 14) to measure whether a 7B-class model actually 
meets the workload’s capability/quality threshold on its evaluation set. Its memory and economics 
are trivially favorable; its fate turns on that measured gate. Because candidate (c) is strongly suspected 
to fail the quality gate [HYPOTHESIS], it is not a finalist; the finalists are Candidates (a) and (b), 
and the near-term decision falls between them. The decision between Candidates (a) and (b) hinges 
on whether the doubled hardware cost of Candidate (b) is justified by its operational flexibility 
(e.g., independent scaling of prefill and decode pools, fault isolation). The architect presents both 
candidates, together with the honest throughput arithmetic of Chapter 8 (no canonical single host 
meets the full prefill demand alone), to the stakeholder as the decision foundation.
