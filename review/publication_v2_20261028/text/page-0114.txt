Inference residency = weights + KV cache; the KV footprint scales with context length. Fine-tuning 
residency adds optimizer states (Adam m, v, and master weights), which for 70B at ~16 bytes/param 
totals ~1,120 GB — roughly 1.75× the 640 GB of 8×H100. This contrast is the architect’s central takeaway: 
inference is memory-limited by weights + KV, while fine-tuning is memory-limited by weights + gradients 
+ optimizer, a substantially higher floor. Note the ~1,120 GB is the parameter/optimizer floor (weights 
+ gradients + Adam m/v/master), which is fixed by model size and precision. The actual training peak 
is higher and is not inferable from that floor alone: it also includes activation memory (scales with batch 
size × sequence length × hidden dim, and is the dominant term for long-context or large-batch runs), 
temporary kernel workspace, allocator fragmentation, and checkpointing buffers. A host that fits the 
1,120 GB floor can still fail to fit a given training run if its activation peak exceeds the remaining HBM — 
so the fit verdict for training must be checked against the measured peak for the specific batch/sequence
