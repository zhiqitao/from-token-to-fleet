At T = 3, β(3) is 1 + 660 ms / 8.6 s ≈ 1.08×, i.e. the orchestration-only component adds roughly 
8% — the agent loop is not “2–6× slower” in the orchestration sense. But β measures only the 
incremental orchestration overhead, not the full agentic task slowdown. The full service time of 
a representative three-turn task grows from ~8.6 s (single-shot) to at least ~13.8 s (~1.6×) once the 
per-turn reasoning decode (~65 γ tokens/turn) and the longer grown-context prefill are counted — 
a full increase of ~5.2–5.6 s. So β is βorch (orchestration-only), and the true task-level multiplier is 
Ltask E2E/Lbase ≈1.6–1.65× at T = 3. The bound that actually matters for the architect is that the 
added orchestration overhead per turn is ~220 ms (and must be budgeted), while the total request 
time — and hence fleet sizing — is set by the ~13.8 s full service time, not the ~8.6 s single-shot 
generation.
These derived quantities give the architect concrete numbers to set policies: cap T at 3 unless 
the quality gain is statistically significant, and allocate ~220 ms per turn in the orchestration-latency 
budget.
