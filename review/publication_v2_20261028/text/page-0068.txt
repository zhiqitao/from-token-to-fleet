Assumption-change log (how the canonical case evolves). Table 4-2 pins the given 
(assumed workload inputs) and the derived (modeled results) for the canonical scenario. 
As the book proceeds, each chapter adds hypothesized results (a candidate architecture or 
a lever, tagged [HYPOTHESIS] or [ILLUSTRATIVE]) and validated results (a benchmark 
or measurement that confirms or rejects a hypothesis, tagged [LAB] or [2°]). The four 
states — given → derived → hypothesized → validated — are the assumption-change 
log: a reader can always tell whether an 8×H100 host is an assumed baseline, a derived 
lower bound, a hypothesized candidate, or a validated deployment, because the status 
tag and the chapter that introduced it are both visible. The Architecture Decision Record 
(ADR, Ch. 25) is where a hypothesis becomes a validated, owned decision; the evidence 
tags (Preface) are the per-claim record of which state each number is in.
CANONICAL TEACHING MODEL — deliberately conservative full-MHA base­
line. The 70B dense full-MHA model (every query attends to every key) is a teach­
ing/reference model, not a representative 2026 production 70B architecture. Modern 
production serving candidates use GQA/MQA and sparse attention, which shrink the 
KV constant several-fold (Ch 7); the ~2.62 MB/token figure is an upper bound for peda­
gogical clarity, and the frontier appendix (Appendix A) is the deliberate relaxation — 
not a correction — of this deliberately pessimistic baseline. Treating it as a property of 
70B models in general understates 2026 production KV efficiency.
