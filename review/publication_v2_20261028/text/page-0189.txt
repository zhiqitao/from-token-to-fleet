• Goodput — tokens/s that meet the SLO (Ch6), not raw throughput.

The analytical ceiling sets the range of the sweep and flags which levels are physically impossible 
(a single 8×H100 host cannot hold ~344 in-flight requests; that is a fleet burst served by ~20 hosts, 
Ch 16-17). The benchmark then reads off the highest offered load that still meets the SLO — that 
measured SLO-goodput is the provisioning input, not the analytical C/W. The server is loaded at the 
concurrency it can actually hold, and we sweep across that achievable range rather than testing one 
number.
Step 3 — read the result. Suppose candidate A (a 70B dense on 8×H100) shows p95 TTFT 1.9 s 
and a measured goodput that meets the per-request TTFT SLO at the ~17.5-concurrent / ~2.0-req-per-
second analytical load it can hold. The host’s prefill ceiling from Ch 8 is ~19,800 tok/s — the right 
order for the prefill leg of that rate (2.0 × 9,200 = 18,400 input tok/s, Ch17 §17.4.2); the summed ~19K 
figure must not be compared to a prefill-only ceiling (Ch17) — but that is the prefill-only ideal, not 
the goodput: the benchmark’s goodput is the SLO-windowed prefill+decode figure under continuous 
batching, and it is the benchmark (not the ceiling) that confirms the SLO is met at that concurrency. 
Candidate B (a smaller 7B) shows 0.6 s TTFT — much faster — but scores lower on the capability 
screen for the retrieval-QA quality threshold. We therefore select A for deployment (capability + 
deployment both pass), not B (capability fails despite speed). The benchmark did not score one 
number; it reproduced the decision. [ILLUSTRATIVE][DERIVED]
