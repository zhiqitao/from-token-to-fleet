• [UNRESOLVED] An open item that could not be pinned to a first-party source at the time 
of writing. Such items are flagged in the text and must be re-checked against current vendor 
documentation before any procurement or deployment decision; they are precisely the numbers 
most likely to have drifted. In evidence-label lines and copy-ready templates, the parenthetical 
“(to be verified)” is the plain-language form of [UNRESOLVED] and means exactly the same 
thing.
Axis 3 — Measurement status (the measurement-status hierarchy). Where Axis 1 says where 
a number came from and Axis 2 says what it claims to be, Axis 3 says how far it has climbed the 
measurement-status hierarchy — a third, orthogonal axis, folded into this one authoritative taxonomy 
rather than published as a separate scheme. A number in this book can fall into one of four stages, 
and they are not interchangeable. Reading “ceiling” as “achieved” is the single most consequential 
mistake an architect can make with these figures. The same convention is applied in the fleet and 
TCO synthesis tables (Chs. 12, 17, 20, 22).
Stage
What it is / what it is not
ANALYTICAL
an arithmetic or physical upper bound from stated inputs (e.g. KV 
occupancy ceiling C, or the per-host prefill rate). Valid for planning; 
not an achieved rate.
ILLUSTRATIVE
a worked-example value on stated assumptions, chosen to make the 
method concrete; not a measurement or a vendor claim.
MODELED (offered-load)
the result of a simulation or queueing model on a synthetic or 
recorded load curve; reflects the model, not a measured system.
MEASURED 
(SLO-goodput)
the only stage that demonstrates a deployed system meets a rate and 
a tail-latency SLO on real hardware. Requires a load test with a 
recorded trace.
