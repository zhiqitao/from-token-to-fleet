Reading the result. Once the fleet is sized correctly (~20 hosts), the comparison flips entirely 
versus the single-host framing. Self-hosted (~$23.7/1K reqs) is now roughly twice the cloud-scaled 
rate ($11.7/1K) on a fully-loaded basis ($148K/mo vs ~$73K/mo), because the canonical workload 
genuinely needs a multi-host fleet, and self-hosting a 20-host fleet carries ~$116K/mo of capex 
amortization. It is also above the managed-API rate ($20.8/1K), so at this active-hours volume 
self-hosting loses to both the API and scale-to-load cloud. This is an illustrative lower-bound for 
cloud, NOT an apples-to-apples comparison — the $73K cloud figure assumes ideal scale-to-load 
elasticity and a continuously-arriving ~5-host average that bursts briefly to ~20, whereas the $148K 
self-host figure is an always-provisioned 20-host fleet. They do not share the same availability ceiling, 
failover reserve, warm-capacity cost, peak-duration assumption, or p95 SLO. Cloud-scaled being 
cheapest is therefore a lower-bound result of an asymmetric scenario, not a demonstrated 
mode ranking. Without a measured traffic trace and equalized SLO/failover/availability assumptions 
on both sides, the definitive economic winner is not determined.
Apples-to-apples caveat. The $73K cloud figure does not yet include an N−1 failover reserve, 
capacity held for failures/maintenance, warm-capacity cost during idle periods, model-startup time, 
or provider availability variance; the self-host figure does not charge for the same availability target’s 
redundancy. So the two are not compared on equal reliability or load-serving assumptions. 
Before treating cloud as cheaper, add: the same served-load trace, p95 SLO, peak duration, availability 
target, failover coverage, warm capacity, model startup time, and price period to both sides. Under a 
nonideal elastic case (a reserved warm pool sized for N−1 plus slower scale-up), the cloud cost rises 
toward the always-on-at-peak figure and the “cloud is cheapest” result may not hold. As above: the 
$2.8/1K figure is an optimistic lower bound — frame the decision as “cloud is cheaper if scale-up 
is fast and a warm pool can be held cheaply,” not as an unconditional ranking. The decisive caveat 
remains the staff/ops term and whether it is sunk, and now also the fleet requirement — a 
single-host TCO understates the real cost by 5–20×.
