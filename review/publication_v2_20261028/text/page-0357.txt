Caption style convention. A figure caption follows a three-part order — (1) a one-sentence takeaway 
of what the figure shows, (2) a succinct ‘Scope/source:‘ line naming the geometry, assumptions, and 
evidence basis, and (3) any measurement warning — intended to make the figure scannable without 
deleting a qualification. A detailed derivation or cross-chapter reconciliation lives in the section 
body or a nearby note, not in the caption. Where a caption is observed to repeat a body paragraph at 
length, it is shortened to this convention rather than stripped of its provenance/scope condition.
Presentation of evidence tags. Inline provenance and status tags are carried on a number because a 
later calculation or decision depends on it (load-bearing). When the same tag applies to a contiguous 
run of related values or an entire section, the book does not repeat it on every line: it states the tag 
once in a boxed per-section Assumptions & evidence note (or in a table footnote that names the whole 
group), and the reader applies it to every value the note governs. A tag is always shown inline on a 
number that carries a different status from its neighbours, so the reader can spot a status change 
without re-reading the surrounding prose. Never strip a source/status control; group it, so it stays 
legible rather than fragmenting the sentence.
