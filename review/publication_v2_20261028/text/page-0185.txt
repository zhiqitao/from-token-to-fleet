The reference-architecture escalation ladder
escalate only when a constraint binds — else STOP; tiers are orthogonal levels of scale, not a ranking
figure 13.1: The reference-architecture ladder and its escalation triggers. Escalation (red) fires when 
model/KV/throughput outgrows a tier; de-escalation (green) fires on privacy, data sovereignty, and 
cost — but scale-down decisions should also reference utilization (sustained low load on a tier), 
failure domain (a smaller tier has a smaller blast radius), operations burden (fewer hosts to run), 
and SLO margin (headroom to absorb a burst without breaching the SLO). Make these measurable: 
e.g. de-escalate when 30-day p95 utilization < 40% AND SLO margin > 2× AND the smaller tier’s 
failure domain is acceptable.
The reference-architecture ladder: single GPU → single host → multi-host → cluster/fleet, with 
escalation triggers (red: model/KV/throughput outgrows a tier) and de-escalation (green: privacy, data 
sovereignty, cost) moving a workload between tiers.
