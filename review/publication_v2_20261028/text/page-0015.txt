The operational decision checklist
The full reasoning loop is earned in Chapter 22, but its shape is useful from the first page. When 
a workload arrives, the same six questions recur in the same order; the chapters that follow each 
answer one of them. Keep this list in view while reading — it is the spine made operational.
1. What is the workload? — tokens in/out, rps average and peak, concurrency, latency 
SLOs. (Ch. 4)
2. What fits on one host? — weight residency, KV per token, the concurrency floor. 
(Chs. 3, 7)
3. Which phase binds? — prefill compute or decode bandwidth, at this operating point. 
(Chs. 2, 8)
4. How many hosts? — the fleet sized from the per-host bound, with headroom and 
failover. (Chs. 16–17)
5. Who serves it, and how? — batching, parallelism, P/D disaggregation, scheduling. 
(Chs. 10–11, 20)
6. Is it the right call? — TCO, the red-team challenge, the recorded decision. (Chs. 16, 
22, 25)
The six questions are cross-cutting: each is first raised in one part and cashed out across the 
others — Q1 in Part II, Q2–3 across Parts I and III, Q4–5 across Parts III–V, and Q6 in Parts IV and VI. 
The technical justification for why the loop has this shape — and where it breaks — is Chapter 22; 
this checklist is the early, operational view of the same loop.
