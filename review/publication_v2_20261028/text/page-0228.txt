Separate the two questions explicitly — they are not interchangeable, and mixing them is the 
source of the under-sizing error:
1. KV-only concurrency sizing (does one host’s KV budget hold the in-flight requests?): 
NKV = ⌈λW/(C · u)⌉. This uses C (KV-residency ceiling) and the full service time W.
2. Full service-capacity sizing (does the fleet’s aggregate request rate clear the peak?): the 
same quantity when C/W is the per-host service rate — i.e. N = ⌈λ/(ρ · (C/W))⌉. Because 
Little’s Law ties concurrency, service time and arrival rate, these two views coincide: λW/C =
λ/(C/W).
KV residency provides one capacity ceiling, but it is not a fleet-size answer by itself. When the 
canonical 300-token decode duration is included, Little’s Law raises in-flight concurrency from the 
misleading “40 requests at 1 second” approximation to roughly 344 requests at 40 rps, and the fleet 
must be sized to that.
The fleet is governed by the minimum of several independent ceilings, not by KV alone. 
C/W — and the KV-residency ceiling C that feeds it — is a necessary condition, not sufficient under 
mixed prefill/decode scheduling. An architect must take min of: (a) KV-residency concurrency 
C, (b) prefill compute capacity (the ~19,800 tok/s/host prefill screen of Ch. 8), (c) decode band­
width/compute capacity (the HBM-bound decode roofline), and (d) the observed p95-TTFT/TPOT 
SLO goodput under a load test. A host may hold 17.5 KV-resident requests but never serve 17.5 
at the quoted service time, because batch scheduling, prefill/decode interleaving, and tail latency 
couple the two.
