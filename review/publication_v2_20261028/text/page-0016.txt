The Architect’s Evidence Discipline
Every load-bearing quantitative claim in this book carries Axes 1 and 2 as an inline pair; the Axis-3 
measurement status rides along with claims whose measurement status is known, not with every 
claim. Keeping these axes separate is itself a discipline. A claim carries the labels directly (inline, 
e.g. [1P][DERIVED]) or via an identified table or section that states them (e.g. a worked-example 
table whose header or footnote gives the provenance and status for every row). Purely illustrative 
or rhetorical numbers that no later calculation depends on may carry the labels in the surrounding 
prose rather than inline; the Sources, Method, and Reproducibility chapter defines these exceptions 
unambiguously. The provenance axis records where the number came from: [1P] first-party (the 
model vendor’s own spec), [canonical scenario] the book’s own internally defined workload and 
model (Ch. 4, Table 4-2), [ILLUSTRATIVE] a non-canonical worked-example or scenario input 
chosen to make a calculation concrete, [2°] reputable second-hand (textbook, survey, independent 
benchmark), or [LAB] measured on the author’s own home-lab hardware (illustrative of mechanism, 
not production). The epistemic-status axis records what was done to it: FACT directly stated by the 
source, DERIVED computed by arithmetic from stated inputs, ASSUMPTION a deliberately chosen 
scenario input (paired with a scenario provenance — [canonical scenario] or [ILLUSTRATIVE]), 
HYPOTHESIS reasoned but not yet validated, or UNRESOLVED a question the book leaves 
open. A label pair always combines one item from each of the first two axes, e.g. [1P][DERIVED] 
or [ILLUSTRATIVE][ASSUMPTION]; the third axis — measurement status (ANALYTICAL / 
ILLUSTRATIVE / MODELED / MEASURED, or UNMEASURED where the stage is not yet determined) 
— rides with that pair or stands alone, and is defined in full in the Sources, Method, and Reproducibility 
chapter.
One mapping is worth stating explicitly, because it is easy to get wrong: the canonical scenario 
(Ch. 4, Table 4-2) keeps its own provenance. Within that [canonical scenario] provenance the 
epistemic-status axis splits every value into exactly two cases. An input or parameter assumption — a 
traffic level, a latency target, a price, a token count — is [canonical scenario][ASSUMPTION]. 
A value arithmetically derived from those assumed inputs — an execution occupancy, a tokens-per-
second rate, a cost-per-request, a tokens-per-dollar figure — is [canonical scenario][DERIVED]. 
A vendor hardware constant (e.g. the H100 footprint) is [1P][FACT], never [ILLUSTRATIVE]. 
[ILLUSTRATIVE] is reserved for a non-canonical hypothetical worked example (a “what-if” that 
is not the canonical scenario): its inputs are [ILLUSTRATIVE][ASSUMPTION] and its derived 
results [ILLUSTRATIVE][DERIVED]. The ASSUMPTION/DERIVED split tells a reader whether a 
number was chosen or computed; the [canonical scenario]/[ILLUSTRATIVE] split tells them 
whether it is the book’s fixed reference set or a one-off what-if.
