Preface
For a long time I wanted to build the systematic knowledge of what it means to work as an AI 
Solution Architect: how to reason across the layers of an AI system — from tokens and models to 
workloads, systems, infrastructure, and fleets — so that a vague requirement can be turned into a 
defensible architecture. I kept looking for the resource that would lay all of that down in one coherent 
thread. What I found were deep dives into single layers — models, serving, infrastructure, agents — 
but no single spine that connected them into the one thing an architect actually does: chaining a real 
workload to a defensible solution.
That feedback loop — curiosity, question, research, model, write — is the engine of this book: 
writing the reasoning down forces every assumption, quantity, and trade-off to become explicit, and 
exposes the gaps that deserve further measurement. The AI field moves fast, and I am still learning 
too. This handbook is a continuously evolving reference — developed and shared layer by layer, 
decision by decision, from the token up to the fleet. If it helps others build the same systematic 
mental model, then it has outdone what I had planned it for.
Start here. For the working method, begin at Ch. 1 (The Token) and follow the canonical workload 
through Chs. 16–20 (TCO and the fleet, where the numbers pay off), returning to the policy sections 
later. The following Preface sections — the thesis, reading paths, the evidence discipline, and the 
currency policy — are the reference layer: valuable to a careful reader, but skimmable on a first 
pass. The full provenance/taxonomy rule set also lives in the Sources, Method, and Reproducibility 
chapter, so the Preface need not be read in full before starting the chapters.
