# PUBLICATION AUDIT — *From Token to Fleet: An AI Solution Architect's Handbook* (First edition, 2026)

---

## 1. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**What is genuinely strong.** This is not a weak manuscript. It has an unusually clear spine (token → workload → system → architecture → fleet → architect's decision process), a self-imposed two-axis evidence taxonomy that is more disciplined than most comparable books, and a canonical scenario that, for the first time in this book's revision history, is actually carried consistently enough to be checkable. The KV-cache arithmetic — which is the quantity the whole book is built around — is *correct*: `2 × 80 × 8192 × 2 = 2,621,440 B = 2.5 MiB`, 9.5K → 24.9 GB, 640 − 140 − 64 = 436 GB budget, 436 ÷ 24.9037 = 17.51 → conservative 17, FP8 → 32.4. Those figures reconcile across Chs. 7, 15, 17, 19, 25 without drift. The prefill/decode FLOP arithmetic is likewise correct: 1.288 PFLOP at 9.2K, 1.19 PFLOPS required vs 0.989 peak, ridge 989 ÷ 3.35 ≈ 295 FLOP/byte, the quadratic attention term 4·n_layers·L²·d = +17 % at 9.2K / +60 % at 32K / ≈2.4× at 128K — all independently recomputed and confirmed. Ch. 6's insistence that "kernel regime" and "workload capacity deficit" are different claims is a genuine intellectual contribution and is applied consistently in Chs. 2, 4, 6, 8, 15, 20. Ch. 24's insistence that eliminating a candidate is not validating its rival is exactly the right epistemic discipline.

**What is not yet right.** Three families of problem block publication.

*First, a hidden circularity in the fleet-size argument.* Every host count in the book — 5, 11, 20, 29, 143 — derives from one number: `C ≈ 17.5 concurrent requests/host`, which derives from `2.62 MB/token`. That constant is the **full-MHA upper bound**, chosen in Ch. 4 Table 4-3 with the explicit justification that it is "the conservative worst case an architect should always size against first." But "worst case" is not the operating point. The same book tells the reader (Ch. 7, in the very same table) that a GQA 70B carries **0.33 MB/token — 8× less**. Under GQA, C ≈ 140/host, per-host ≈ 16.3 req/s, and the 40 rps peak needs **3 hosts, not 20**. The book therefore contains a *self-validating* cost result: a fleet ~6.7× larger than necessary, priced as the headline TCO conclusion, produced entirely by choosing an upper-bound constant as the canonical operating point and never revisiting the choice when the economics depended on it. This is the single most consequential defect in the manuscript.

*Second, a uniform whitespace/scale failure across nearly every diagram.* Every chapter figure sits in the upper half of its page with 300–500 px of dead space below and none above; two figures (7.4, 20.1) are clipped at the right edge by the trim. Fig. 8.1 has five of seven boxes with text overflowing their borders, one box unreadable at print size. Fig. 1.2 has four text collisions. Fig. 13.1 has four trigger labels running through the arrow shaft. Fig. 12.1's gate logic (all candidates fail, yet (a) and (b) are shown as "SURVIVORS") contradicts Ch. 12's own table. These are not aesthetic notes; at printed size several figures carry no information at all.

*Third, unearned precision in the load-bearing economic figures.* Fig. 17.1 plots a 5.5 req/s burst — a number that appears nowhere in the canonical scenario, derived nowhere, and contradicted by the chapter's own λ·W = 344 → ≈20 hosts result on the same page. Fig. 16.1 draws a "break-even" between two flat lines, which geometrically cannot produce one, and whose 40 rps annotations contradict the tables directly beneath it. Fig. 20.1's 95 % efficiency loss and hard plateau are presented as "measured" with no measurement. Precision of this kind is worse than vagueness, because it transfers false authority to the reader.

**Bottom line:** the technical core is sound and the architecture is right; the presentation and the constant-selection discipline are not yet at publication standard. Roughly 6 figures require redesign, 12 require major revision, and one structural decision (the canonical KV constant) requires re-derivation of every downstream economic result.

## 2. HIGHEST-PRIORITY TECHNICAL / CONCEPTUAL ISSUES

**P0-1 — The canonical KV constant is an upper bound, and the entire cost model rests on it.**
Ch. 4 Table 4-3 pins the canonical scenario to `KV per token (FP16, MHA) = 2.62 MB` and Ch. 4's box calls FP16 2.62 MB/token "the canonical scenario's KV constant." Table 7-1 and Table 7-2 (same page spread as the GQA paragraph) carry `~0.33 MB/token, ~3 GB at 9.2K` for GQA. Consequences, none surfaced:
- C: 17.5 → ~140 concurrent/host (436 ÷ 3.0 GB).
- Per-host throughput: 2.0 → 16.3 req/s.
- Fleet at 40 rps peak: 20 → **~3 hosts**.
- At the 10 rps average: 5 → **1 host** (16.3 > 10).
- Ch. 16 TCO: self-hosted fleet ~$148K/mo → ~$32–40K/mo, which **inverts the chapter's headline conclusion** that "self-host loses to scale-to-load cloud."
This is not a footnote. It is the difference between a self-hosted recommendation and a cloud recommendation for the book's own canonical workload.
**Required action:** either (a) split the canonical scenario into an explicit *teaching* configuration (MHA bound, used for the arithmetic pedagogy of Chs. 3, 7, 8) and an *operating* configuration (GQA, used for every capacity, fleet, cost and TCO result from Ch. 12 onward), or (b) keep the MHA bound as canonical and *own* the consequence — state in Ch. 4 and Ch. 16 that the canonical cost is a worst-case bound, roughly 6–7× a GQA deployment, and that the recommendation therefore holds only for full-MHA-class models. Option (a) is stronger and costs about one new column and a re-run of five tables.

**P0-2 — `W ≈ 8.6 s` is derived from an SLO *budget*, then used as a *service time*.**
`W = 1.08 s prefill + 300 × 25 ms = 8.6 s` treats the TPOT budget as the achieved TPOT. But the decode-bandwidth arithmetic the book itself supplies shows that at C = 17.5-way concurrency the achievable per-step time is `140 GB ÷ (8 × 3.35 TB/s) ≈ 5.2 ms`, i.e. TPOT ≈ 5.2 ms and `W ≈ 2.6 s`. Every fleet and TCO number in the book sits between these two poles:

| assumed TPOT | decode W | total W | in-flight @ 40 rps | hosts (C=17.5, full util) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5.2 ms (bandwidth-implied) | 1.6 s | 2.6 s | 105 | ~6 |
| 10 ms | 3.0 s | 4.1 s | 164 | ~10 |
| 25 ms (book's budget) | 7.5 s | 8.6 s | 344 | ~20 |

The book's headline figures use only the top row of that range, which is the *least* favourable and is not derived from any measurement. **Required action:** state explicitly which TPOT the fleet number assumes, add the sensitivity band as a first-class result in Ch. 17 Table 17-1, and re-word every downstream "≈20 hosts" and "$148K/mo" to carry its assumption. This is the second-biggest credibility risk in the book after the KV constant, because a reader who computes the decode bound will find the fleet 3× oversized and will not know why.

**P0-3 — The derived-vs-measured boundary collapses at the fleet level.**
Ch. 20's Fig. 20.1 draws a line labelled `MEASURED deployment throughput` that is, by the book's own caption, "the ~2.0 req/s/host bound reduced by ~95 % scheduling efficiency," with a hard plateau at 40 rps and the annotation `~5 hosts → ~9.5 req/s`. There is no measurement anywhere in the book. Ch. 20's text says explicitly that ~2.0 req/s "must come from a deployment benchmark." Presenting a modelled, unmeasured, arbitrary derate as "MEASURED" in a bold 12 pt legend is the most serious evidence-labelling defect in the manuscript and is a P0 on its own. **Required action:** relabel `MEASURED` → `ILLUSTRATIVE MODEL`, and either supply the benchmark or delete the axis.

**P0-4 — Fig. 8.1 is partially unreadable at print size and physically broken.**
The "HBM (GPU memory) large · slower" box has three lines of text overflowing its top and bottom borders; the line "what runs here →" collides with the "registers" box's caption; the "tensor cores / ALU" box has a text fragment ("tesf" — evidently a clipped "fastest · closest") sitting on top of it. The right-hand vertical annotation ("capacity grows / speed drops / distance grows") overlaps the "L2 cache" row's description. This is the chapter's anchor figure for the book's central claim that compute, bandwidth, capacity and interconnect are *distinct* constraints, and a reader cannot parse it without zooming. **REDESIGN required.**

**P0-5 — Fig. 12.1 encodes the wrong verdict and is internally contradictory.**
The rendered figure shows candidates (a) and (b) under a green "SURVIVORS" box, while the manuscript's own caption says (a) "fails throughput" and (b) "fails throughput at a single host." A reader looking at the figure gets the opposite of the chapter's conclusion. The `OUTCOME` stage also has no incoming connector from `CANDIDATES`. **Required action:** redesign as a 3 × 3 gate matrix (rows = candidates, columns = MEM/LAT/ECON/THROUGHPUT/QUALITY, cells = ✓/✗ with the arithmetic in-cell), because that is the actual information content and Table 12-1 already exists in that form.

**P0-6 — Fig. 7.4 is clipped at the right trim.**
The "436 GB KV budget" segment and its label run off the page edge; the "640 GB" annotation is cut; the "~64 GB runtime" label is half-swallowed by the grey segment boundary. A clipped figure is a defect, not a preference.

**P0-7 — Fig. 17.1's burst (5.5 req/s) contradicts the chapter it illustrates.**
Ch. 17 §3 derives ~20 hosts from λ·W = 344. Fig. 17.1 sizes a **4-host** fleet against a **5.5 req/s** burst. Both numbers are unsourced; the figure's own caption calls 5.5 req/s "the burst," which is a workload statement, not a measurement. A reader who trusts the figure will under-provision by 5×. **Required action:** redraw at the canonical 40 rps with the λ·W derivation shown as the geometry (burst × W → in-flight → ÷ C → hosts), which is the figure's actual intellectual content.

## 3. STORYLINE / VALUE-BASED REVIEW

**What works.** The Part structure is earned. Part I (Chs. 1–3) establishes units, mechanism and parameter regime before any capacity claim. Part III (Chs. 7–11) is the best sequence in the book: memory → compute → communication → parallelism → serving, each consuming the previous one's output. Ch. 12 (synthesize) and Ch. 14 (select) bracket Chs. 13/15/16 correctly. The Preface's two reading paths (layer-by-layer and question-by-question) are a genuine aid and are honestly stated as independent.

**Value problems, in order of severity.**

1. **Ch. 22 (`How to Think Like an AI Solution Architect`) is the book's thesis chapter and is 80 % redundant with Ch. 1 + Ch. 4 + Table 22-1.** Its §1 re-teaches the token (explicitly conceding "We do not restate that here" and restating it), §2 re-teaches the prefill/decode split ("we do not re-derive that distinction; we use it"), §4 is a near-verbatim copy of Ch. 1 §4 with different bullet wording, §5 is a near-verbatim copy of Ch. 1 §5 with a fifth added bullet, and §8's mini-case is Ch. 1 §8 and Ch. 2 §8 fused. Its one genuinely new artifact — Table 22-1 — is a subset of Table 1-1 plus two rows. Meanwhile the Preface tells readers to "start with Ch. 22" for practising architects, which means the recommended entry point is the chapter that repeats the most. **Recommendation:** collapse Ch. 22 to §3 (the canonical loop, ~1,200 words) + Table 22-1 + the hierarchy line (Requirement → Workload → Intelligence → System → Architecture → Fleet → Decision), which is its unique contribution, and promote it into the Preface as a boxed "the decision loop" spread. Move §7 (What We Still Don't Know) out — it is four pointers to Ch. 2 §7.

2. **Ch. 21 (`The AI Factory`) is largely generic MLOps.** Of its eight sections, §1, §2, §4, §5, §7 and §8 are release-engineering practice that does not depend on AI architecture: a CI/CD gate with a canary, a rollback plan, MTTR. Nothing in the chapter uses a token, a FLOP, a KV byte or an SLO. Its one quantitative result (the 216K-vs-3.6K blast-radius arithmetic) is a percentage-times-a-day calculation. Meanwhile it is positioned as the *synthesis* of Part V, which over-promises. **Recommendation:** either demote it to a section inside Ch. 26 (toolkit) or rewrite §3–§8 around what is AI-specific: promotion gates that must include TTFT/goodput *and* quality together (because a model swap changes both and only one is usually gated), KV-cache-format versioning as a breaking change (FP16 → FP8 KV invalidates every warm cache and every prefix-cache hit), tokenizer-change detection (a tokenizer upgrade silently changes token counts, therefore KV sizing, therefore prefill FLOPs, therefore fleet size — a dependency no other chapter in the book draws), and fleet-wide canary semantics under P/D disaggregation. That version would advance the spine; the current one does not.

3. **Ch. 13 (`Reference Architectures: From Local to Hyperscale`) is the weakest chapter in Part IV.** §1 and §2 are four sentences of taxonomy; §3 is a 4-row table; §4–§7 are generic measurement advice and unknowns; §8 is a restatement of Ch. 20's material. Its stated question ("place it on the right archetype") is answered by a table whose rows are: 1 GPU / 1–8 GPUs / tens–hundreds / dedicated. That is a definition, not a derivation. It also has no figure of its own that carries information (Fig. 13.1 has four text-through-arrow collisions and a 480 px blank gutter). **Recommendation:** merge §1–§3 into Ch. 12 as a "tier feasibility screen" (the three triggers — residency, goodput ceiling, interconnect fraction — are genuinely useful and are stated there already), and keep only the escalation/de-escalation decision rule, promoted into Ch. 12's candidate-synthesis method. That frees a chapter slot for the tier screen's missing content: an explicit per-tier budget table (GPUs, aggregate HBM, fabric BW, per-tier $/token at the canonical token profile) with the canonical workload's position marked.

4. **Ch. 24 (`Red Team / Green Team`) has the lowest evidence quality in the book and does not advance the spine.** PIES = 217 is derived from an author-chosen deduplication threshold (NLE ≤ 0.25) applied to a hand-written combinatorial enumeration, and the chapter is admirably honest that this is a toy metric. But the honest framing does not rescue it: no reader makes an architecture decision from PIES. The poisoned-hit-rate null model (52 expected hits per 10,000 queries under uniform retrieval) is explicitly labelled unrealistic. Every threshold in Table 24-1 is author-invented. Chapter 24 is currently an exercise in security-metric hygiene wearing an architecture chapter's clothes. **Recommendation:** (a) move the PIES material to an appendix as a *method illustration* with its full caveats, (b) keep in the body only the one part that is genuinely architectural — §3.3, the Red-Team attack on the *architecture decision* (the 4×/8× node comparison overturned on peak prefill compute, failure domain, and utilization-is-not-the-point). That §3.3 is excellent: it is the only place in the book where a candidate architecture is destroyed by adversarial reasoning, it correctly distinguishes "eliminated A" from "validated B," and it correctly computes the 6.5-host theoretical floor and the 16.3-host 40 %-MFU floor. **Promote §3.3 to a full chapter or fold it into Ch. 24's opening as the chapter's spine**, and demote the PIES/correlation tables.

5. **Ch. 3's §6 ("The 2026 baseline shift") is 900 words of frontier snapshot inside the model-understanding chapter**, and it is delivered a second time, at greater length, in Appendix A. The book itself says so ("Appendix A documents these at full depth with first-party provenance; what matters here… is that this is not a competing mechanism"). Two placements is one too many. **Recommendation:** reduce Ch. 3 §6 to a 150-word pointer with the framework statement (total-vs-active, KV-function-not-constant, re-derive the roofline), and let Appendix A own the vendor material.

6. **Ch. 6 §"Detecting the Regime" duplicates Ch. 2 §3 and Ch. 8 §3, then re-litigates the same 5.6-vs-3.35 and 1.19-vs-0.989 comparisons a third and fourth time.** The book is careful to re-scope each ("single-device capacity illustration, not the kernel regime"), which is good, but the repetition of the *same two numbers* across four chapters with progressively longer disclaimers is a symptom of the derivation not having been placed once. **Recommendation:** state the decode bandwidth arithmetic once, in Ch. 8 (where the ridge point is defined), with a per-GPU/per-host/aggregate scope table. Everywhere else, cite the scope, not the number.

7. **Ch. 26 §2.1's matrix is the book's best single-page artifact and is buried.** Ten workload families × dominant constraint × first strategy to evaluate, with chapter pointers. It belongs in the Preface as the reader's routing table (the Preface currently promises "Chapter 26 (§2.1) carries a workload-to-strategy decision matrix" as a "quick reference" — a promise the reader must turn 265 pages to collect).

8. **Ch. 20 largely re-derives Ch. 17.** The per-host 2.0 req/s, the C/W identity, the "two capacities must be reconciled" argument, and the 8→16-host marginal cost all appear in Ch. 17 §3/§4. What Ch. 20 uniquely owns — the *infeasible-SLO* insight (a <100 ms end-to-end p99 is unachievable by 2 orders of magnitude; the only controllable quantity is scheduler-added latency) — is buried in §8 and never elevated. **Recommendation:** keep in Ch. 20 only (a) the marginal-cost-per-added-host algebra, (b) the infeasible-SLO analysis as §3, (c) fleet-level autoscaling policy and data colocation. Delete the rest as Ch. 17 cross-references.

9. **Ch. 23 is correct, necessary, and thin.** Six questions, a mirror test, a two-window honesty rule. It is ~1,500 words of professional practice with no new mechanism. That is appropriate for the chapter's purpose, but it should be candidly labelled as practice rather than dressed as architecture — currently its §3 dialogue imports *different* numbers from the canonical scenario without saying so (see §4 below), which makes it read as architecture while it is sales engineering.

10. **The Part V → Part VI transition is asserted rather than earned.** Ch. 21 §9 says the next chapters "formalize the loop that has quietly run through every chapter." They largely re-run it instead (Ch. 22). Fixing items 1 and 2 fixes this.

**One arithmetic error found.** Fig. A.2 reports GLM-5.3-Flash as **KV ~23 %, FLOP ~33 %**. ### 4c. Terminology audit

**Correct and consistently held** (and worth protecting): compute-bound / bandwidth-bound / capacity-constrained (Ch. 8's explicit three-way box); kernel regime vs workload capacity deficit (Chs. 2, 6, 8, 15, 20); residency floor vs practical host requirement (Ch. 7); parameterized-linear FLOP floor vs total prefill FLOP; logical footprint vs physical traffic vs canonical weight-stream approximation (Ch. 2 — this is one of the best passages in the book); offered load vs hardware utilization (Ch. 24's point 3); aggregate vs per-rank (Fig. 7.4/7.5 captions); incremental orchestration overhead vs full agentic service time (Ch. 19's scope note, carried into Ch. 17); per-model nominal vs fleet-amortized cost basis (Ch. 18's basis note); DERIVED vs MEASURED vs ILLUSTRATIVE for Fig. 9.1, Fig. A.1, Fig. A.2.

**Drift found.**

1. **MB vs MiB is not enforced.** The book computes 2,621,440 B = 2.5 MiB = 2.62 MB and is careful about this in Ch. 7 and Ch. 22. But then says "the book quotes **~2.5 MB/token**" (Ch. 7 §4), which is wrong — the book quotes 2.62 MB and calls 2.5 MB its shorthand. Fix the sentence.
2. **"host" vs "node" vs "server".** The Preface's reading conventions do not define these. "8×H100 host" and "8×H100 server" are used interchangeably in Ch. 16 ($350K per "fully built 8×H100 server" → $5.8K/month per "host") without ever stating they are the same object. Ch. 9 says "nodes within a server," implying node ⊂ server, contradicting "host." **Define once: host = the 8-GPU NVLink-connected box; use it everywhere.**
3. **"rps" vs "QPS".** Ch. 14's Step 2 says "not merely target the same average QPS." One occurrence of an undefined synonym.
4. **"goodput" is used loosely in three places.** Ch. 14 Step 3 calls ~19,800 tok/s "the host's prefill ceiling from Ch. 8" and then calls the same number "goodput" — but 19,800 tok/s is a *token* rate (mostly prefill tokens), while goodput is defined in Ch. 6 as SLO-meeting tokens. A prefill token rate cannot be goodput. Ch. 20 does this correctly ("do NOT divide ~35,000 tokens/s by ~9,500 tokens/request"); Ch. 14 does not.
5. **"decode" is used for both the phase and the output stage.** Ch. 15 §2 is careful to define a 5-phase pipeline including "detokenization/streaming" as distinct from decode, but Ch. 1, 2, 8, 22 use "decode" only for the phase. Harmonise.
6. **"prompt caching" is defined in Ch. 2's vocabulary table as meaning *the same thing* as prefix caching** ("the book uses it to mean reusing the KV of a shared prompt prefix across requests"). A vocabulary table that defines term A as identical to term B teaches the reader that A is redundant. Either delete "prompt caching" or define it as the provider-marketing umbrella that *contains* prefix caching.
7. **"expert parallelism" in Ch. 10's Table 10-1 says it "replicates attention / weights"** — EP replicates attention and dense weights, shards experts. Correct, but the table cell reads ambiguously against the TP row. Clarify.
8. **"self-host cost per million tokens" (Ch. 4) vs "tokens per dollar-hour" (Ch. 5) vs "$/tok/s" (Ch. 5).** The Ch. 5 note "the effective cost is ~0.40 $/tok/s" is a dimensionally odd construct that a reader will not parse. Convert to $/M tokens throughout, matching Ch. 4 and Ch. 16.

### 4d. Evidence and citation quality

**Strengths.** The two-axis taxonomy ([1P]/[2°]/[LAB]/[ILLUSTRATIVE] × [FACT]/[DERIVED]/[ASSUMPTION]/[HYPOTHESIS]) is defined in the Preface, applied in the tables, and — importantly — *never gamed*: Ch. 12's prefix-cache amortization is labelled [HYPOTHESIS] while sitting in a paragraph that wants it to be true; Ch. 25's ADR 0016 explicitly labels its quality comparison as an assumed outcome "not a measurement in this handbook"; Ch. 24's PIES explicitly labels itself a toy metric with a stated threshold. This is rarer than it should be.

**Problems.**

1. **`[LAB]` is defined in the Preface and used nowhere.** Either use it (with a genuine home-lab measurement) or remove it from the taxonomy.
2. **`[2°]` is used for things that are not second-hand.** Ch. 4 Table 4-3 labels the canonical 9,200/300 token profile `[ILLUSTRATIVE][ASSUMPTION]`, correct — but Ch. 5's 1,200 prompt is `[1P] canonical scenario (Ch 4, Table 4-3)` and Ch. 2 Table 2-1 labels the FP16 weight bytes `[ILLUSTRATIVE][DERIVED]`. 4. **Two `\needspace{...}` / `:::` markdown directives survived** — Ch. 5's `\needspace{250pt}` and Ch. 22's `::: {#tab-22-1}` wrapper. Raw build-tool syntax on p. 60 and p. 235.

6. **The front-matter roadmap contains at least three stale pointers:** "token → cost Ch1" (Ch. 1 prices *tokens*; cost is Ch. 16); "who serves it? Ch18" (Ch. 18 is multi-model routing; serving is Ch. 11); "is it the right call? Ch21/26" (Ch. 21 is the factory, Ch. 26 is the toolkit). Fix.
7. **Chapter 13's title does not match its content.** It is titled `Reference Architectures` but §3 is entirely "Placing the Canonical Workload." Either rename to *Deployment Tiers: From Local to Hyperscale* or rewrite §3.
8. **Appendix A's citation keys are arXiv-style identifiers for 2026 papers** (`arXiv 2606.19348`, `arXiv 2607.24653`, `arXiv 2602.15763`). These encode YYMM = 2026-06, 2026-07, 2026-02.
